# Railways: routes of the mod's own in use (cutting, switching, cost routes)

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`); how a route is given to the autopilot is in `domains/railroad/own-routes.md`. Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

- **[source]** `FTrainAtcData::ClearPath()` (`FGTrain.h:174`) is public and drops the train's current route; a mod that ends a route of its own can call it so the game finds its own route again (in the second tick, as after a load). Its effect in the packaged game is not observed yet. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A route to a signal written with `SetPath` while the train rolls is accepted the same way as one written while it stands: after the autopilot was switched off and on at 29 km/h, the game found its own route in the next tick, the mod wrote its route again (`route … written (the game replaced the route): 14 points, 488.1 m, at 29 km/h, SetPath 1`) and the train stopped 8.4 m before the signal. A mod route that branches off the game's route must be compared point by point with the game's route (the common prefix must end before the game's route does): the switch's own curve track lies between the game's route and the siding's signal block, so a check by tracks ("every track of the way is on the route or in the siding's block") refuses a correct cut. Evidence: with the track check no cut happened on three saves (`cannot stop at … any more`), with the prefix check `route of train … cut at <signal>, 413 m before the stop point at 26 km/h, braking distance 10 m` and the train stopped there. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A train that was moving when the game was saved comes back moving after the load; with its autopilot switched off in the mod's first frame it kept rolling (29 km/h at the load, 25 km/h when the autopilot went on again a few seconds later) instead of braking. **[?]** Whether it would brake to a stop on its own with the autopilot off for longer was not watched. Evidence: `after the load: <train> at 29 km/h, autopilot 0, run yes, phase 3, graph 0`, then `route of train … written (the game found its route): 10 points, 375.3 m, at 25 km/h`. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A move to a parking signal still needs a timetable stop, a station the autopilot drives towards. The mod's own route then replaces the game's path and ends at the signal. The stop must lie the way the train sets off on the own route. A train with a locomotive at each end takes its direction from the game's path to the stop. If the stop lies behind the train, for example a train that stopped just past the switch of a dead-end siding, the game drives the other way, and no route to the siding starts from the first path point. Pick the stop as follows. Search from the train to the siding (as the game would) and keep the first path connection. Accept only a station whose game route from the train starts at that same connection. If the only station past the switch is the one the train stands at, which happens on a terminal line with a spur just before the end, search from where the train stands instead. Evidence: <scenario> with the train switched off past the switch: before the fix `move 2 ... ends where the train is: no way from where the train is to the signal`, after it `move 2 done, train <train> stands at parking signal <signal>`. A train at the terminal station: before the fix `move 1 ... refused: no station is reachable past the siding`, after it parked. (2026-10-12, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route can be switched to another branch under a moving train, 150 m before the switch at 92 km/h, and the signals follow the new route.** A train cruising on the game's own route towards a path signal at stop (the block behind the junction held a standing train) got a route of the mod's over the siding instead, written with `mAtcData.SetPath` from the connection ahead of it (the game's path point at `CurrentPathSegment`): `SetPath` returned true, `CurrentPathSegment` went to 0 and was right again on the next frame, the path signal cleared for the new route, the train slowed from 92 to 48 km/h (it had been braking for the red signal: braking distance 90 m at the change) and accelerated again on the siding without stopping, drove all 9 tracks of the siding and docked at its stop 31 s later; no autopilot error, no route dropped. The fork for "N m before the switch" is the last point the old and the new route share (`DistanceToM` along the current route from `CurrentConnectionDistance` and the points' `Distance`). Evidence: `S9 route changed on <train> 149.8 m before the fork at 92 km/h, braking distance 90 m …: 17 points, 472.1 m (the game's 23 points, 696.1 m)`, `S9 route written … SetPath 1, segment 10 -> 0, at 92 km/h`, `<train> is past the fork at 90 km/h; the lowest speed since the change 48 km/h`, `docked at <station> after 53.1 s … the mod's own tracks driven 9 of 9`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route cut at one parking signal can be re-cut at another siding of the same ladder while the train runs, 100 m before the switch at 69 km/h, after the path signal at that switch has already cleared for the first route.** A train on a route of the mod's that ended at the exit signal of siding 1 (two sidings side by side between one pair of switches) got, 99.7 m before the point where the two ways part (braking distance 55 m, the path signal at the switch `RSA_Clear` for the first route), the game's own search from the connection ahead of it to the guarded connection of siding 2's exit signal, written with `SetPath` (`segment 9 -> 0`); it stopped 8.0 m before that signal 19.6 s later with every vehicle in siding 2's block, no autopilot error, the game's own pathfinding not run again. So a siding can be chosen or changed on the way as long as the fork is still ahead; the game re-reserves the junction for the new route. Evidence: `S9 target changed from <signal 1> to <signal 2> on <train> 99.7 m before the fork at 69 km/h, braking distance 55 m, the signal at the fork <path signal> shows RSA_Clear`, `S9 route written … (on the way, cut at signal 2): 6 points, 176.2 m, SetPath 1, segment 9 -> 0, at 69 km/h`, `stopped 8.0 m before parking signal <signal 2> … 19.6 s after the change, whole train in its block 1, … autopilot errors 0, the game's routes 1`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- A train with a timetable written by a test counts as a player's train for a mod that reads timetables (nothing in `AFGRailroadTimeTable` says who wrote the stops): a test that gave two trains a stop to clear a siding made the mod's parking checks refuse that siding at once (`parking signal … is not usable any more: The player's train <train> drives through here`). Such a train stops counting as a player's train only after the test has cleared the stops again (`SetStops({})`, autopilot off) and the checks have run again. Evidence: the two alarm lines 0.5 s after the stops were set in a test run. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A cost detour must not run through a parking siding or another station's platform; in production the cost route to a station works through docking and loading.** A mod that writes its own cost route (the game's A* with a cost filter, written with `mAtcData.SetPath` at the frame the game computed its route) gave a train a detour 10 m longer round a signal block with a moving train; the detour ran through a siding the mod uses as a parking place, the moving train parked there a moment later (autopilot off), and the detouring train stood behind it at the signal for good: `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` after 300 s, two more trains queued behind it. The game recomputes a route only after the autopilot goes on, after docking, after a stop change and after a load, never while a train waits at a signal, so nothing breaks such a wait. What helped: in the filter's `IsTraversalAllowed`, refuse the tracks of every siding the mod parks trains on and every platform's track (`AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetIsOwnedByPlatform()`) unless the game's own route drives that track already; then the detour goes round on plain track only, and the train's own way out of a siding or through a station on its route stays open. Also: a train standing still must never cost less than a moving one, or a detour round a moving train can end behind a stopped one. With these rules a route written this way (ending at the station's stopping connection, `Path->Station` copied from the game's route) was driven, docked, loaded at the first station and went on to the second one normally (job done, 9 of 9 detour tracks driven, at most 1.5 s at a signal). Evidence: log lines `cost route of <train> to <station A> written: 31 points, 1076.3 m instead of the game's 1066.9 m`, the parking train's last vehicle on the very track the detouring train's front then stood on, the detouring train `at signal 316 s` with `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal`; after the change the same queue scenario passed with no detour written and a dedicated scenario with the siding switched on as a parking place wrote 0 detours. (2026-10-16, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A cost route re-searched on the way once a cycle switches a moving train to a parallel track before a train that entered the main line ahead, but a trigger on every rise of the penalties also reacts to trains that only pass through.** A route watcher that sums the per-block penalties of other trains on the tracks ahead of the front once a cycle and runs a new cost search (connection under the front to the same station stop) when the sum rises, writing the result only with the fork ahead by at least `mAtcData.BrakeDistance`, switched a train at 70 km/h 247 m before the fork (braking distance 72 m) to the parallel track; its lowest speed afterwards was 27.2 km/h, it drove 5 of 5 detour tracks and finished the delivery. On the leg before, the same trigger first wrote a detour at departure and 8.5 s later wrote the game's route back, because a moving train drove into one of the detour's blocks; 8 cost searches gave 3 writes on two legs. **[?]** A rise from a standing train (below 10 cm/s, the block will not clear by itself) is the reliable trigger; a rise from a moving train is worth one write a leg at most. Evidence: `cost route of <train> to <station> written (the route ahead got dearer): 16 points, 584.8 m instead of 560.1 m … 247 m before the switch with 72 m of braking distance, at 70 km/h, SetPath 1`, `cost route … written (the route ahead got dearer): 30 points, 940.1 m instead of 1032.2 m … at 53 km/h` 8.5 s after `… written: 34 points, 1032.2 m instead of 940.1 m … at 0 km/h`. (2026-10-16, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route cut to a siding off a route of the mod's own is built from the train's current path points up to the switch connection in front of the siding, plus the game's search from that connection into the siding.** After a cost route had left the game's route before the siding, a cut that searched from the train (the game's filter) found no way that branches off the route ahead (`too late to cut the route`). Taking the current path points from `CurrentPathSegment` up to the connection the siding's switch leaves from (known from the track graph), appending the search from that connection to the guarded connection of the siding's exit signal, and measuring the room as the difference of the path points' `Distance` plus the search's distance gave `route of <train> cut at the siding …, 639 m before the stop point`; the train stopped 8.4 m before the signal, whole in the siding, and never entered the main track the cost route avoided. Evidence: those two lines and `stopped at the siding at … 8.4 m before the end of its route`. (2026-10-16, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **[?] A cost filter that prices whole signal blocks finds no detour round a train standing inside a junction's path block: every branch of that junction runs through the same occupied block.** A mod priced each track by the game's block it lies in (2000 m when another train stands in the block) and searched the game's A* with that filter. A blocker train was sent to the exit signal at the end of the first branch on its route (a siding beside a one-way track of a double-track line, path signals before its switch and its merge, block signals behind them); it stopped with part of the train still in the path block of the switch. The train behind waited at the path signal in front of the switch ("its block (path) occupied by <blocker>") for 810 s; the cost search returned the game's own route (0 cost routes written), because the main track and the siding both start inside that one priced block. On a single-track loop with a passing track whose main part has its own block the same filter routed round a standing train every time. Whether the game's path signal would have let a train in on the siding's path past the blocker was not tested. A test that needs a detour should place the blocker wholly inside a block of the branch, behind the block signal after the switch. (2026-10-17, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A price on the tracks of players' trains' routes, as a share of each track's length, changes about a sixth of the station-to-station routes on a loop network once detours may not use foreign platforms or parking sidings.** With 100% of the length added on the 46 tracks of one player's timetable route, 15 of 90 routes between station pairs differed from the game's (0 through a platform the game's route does not pass); with a flat 100 m per track and no closed platforms or sidings the same network gave 45 of 90. The filter cost 9.1 µs per search on average against 9.2 µs for the game's own filter. A share of the length suits better than a sum per track, because players build the same stretch from one long track or many short ones. (2026-10-17, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A price on stations a mod's trains should keep out of changes a route only where the way round is more than 100 m longer than the track through the station, because the game's own search already takes every shorter way round.** A mod's filter around `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter` added a fixed 2000 m to the building track of each station it wanted avoided (not on the route's goal track), with detours allowed only over plain track and platforms the game's route passes. On ten test networks whose stations either had a bypass of about their own length or none, 0 station-to-station routes changed: the game already drove the bypasses (station plus its 100 m surcharge is dearer) and the stations without one had no way round. After a bypass 19.5 m longer than the track through `<station>` was rebuilt to 107 m longer, 21 of 37 routes through such a station took it, 0 kept a station they could have avoided, and with the price at 0 every route equalled the game's. A train on such a route drove the bypass and never stood on the station's track (written route 14 points, 415.4 m instead of 308.0 m). On a player's save of 1830 tracks and 19 stations, all priced, 3 of 342 pairs changed, to ways 0.8–1.8 km longer; the priced search took 114 µs on average and at most 147 µs (the game's filter 109 µs over all pairs). **[?]** A search whose game route passes such a station with no way round still explores every node cheaper than the route plus the price, so the price also bounds the cost of that search. Evidence: `quarantine: 37 game routes through a station without a role, 21 avoided, 28 still through one: 28 with no way round, 0 with a dearer way round, 0 with a cheaper way round; 0 routes dearer than the game's`, `Driver: cost route of train <train> to <station> written: 14 points, 415.4 m instead of 308.0 m`, `the runner never drove onto <station>'s track ... seen there 0 time(s)`. (2026-10-17, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
