# Railways: route search and what the autopilot does with a route

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

## Routes, signals, blocks

- `FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync(AFGLocomotive*, AFGBuildableRailroadStation*, FRailroadGraphAStarFilter)` (`RailroadNavigation.h`): native A*, synchronous. A mod can call it, and the target is a station. The locomotive does not turn around: the search goes only forwards from the locomotive it is given (see "Push-pull trains" below).
  - Called from mod code for a parked train with the autopilot off and no timetable, it returned `RPFR_Success` with a 4-point, 48.0 m route to a station the train had never been sent to. The train stayed untouched: `mAtcData.Path` was still empty afterwards.
  - It gives the route the autopilot would take, so there is no need to rebuild the game's cost function. That was checked for a standing train with no timetable (the call below) and in the push-pull runs; the cost of the call and a query between two stations without a train at the first one are measured in the next entries. Evidence (game 502094): `asked the game directly for a route …: RPFR_Success, 4 points, 48.0 m …; route on the train afterwards: no path`.
  - A route handed to the autopilot may end at a signal (tested) or a station, not only at a station; **[?]** a switch connection without a signal was not tried. It must end on such a connection, not on plain track (see "A route given to the autopilot has to end at a connection it steers by" below).
- **The game's route search can be run from any track connection with public parts, no locomotive and no access transformer needed.** `FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync` takes a locomotive and its inner `FindPathSyncInternal` is private, but the pieces it is built from are public and exported: `FRailroadGraphAStarHelper` (the graph), `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter` (costs and the signal rule) and the engine's `FGraphAStar` template (`GraphAStar.h`, module `AIModule`). `FGraphAStar<FRailroadGraphAStarHelper> Search(Helper); Search.FindPath(FRailroadGraphAStarPathPoint(Start), FRailroadGraphAStarPathPoint(Goal), FRailroadGraphAStarFilter(), Points)` with `Start` = the connection at the far end of the track a locomotive stands on (`Track->GetConnection(Position.Forward > 0 ? 1 : 0)` of its `GetTrackPosition()`) and `Goal` = the target station's stop connection (the connection of the station's own track whose `GetStation()` is the station) returned the same result as `FindPathSync` for every locomotive and every station of four track networks: 35 questions, 28 routes of 15.6 points on average, every point the same, distances equal (the sum of `GetLength()` of the tracks of points 1..n is the game's `PathPoints[0].Distance`), and `GoalUnreachable` exactly where the game said `RPFR_Unreachable`. So a mod can ask for the game's route between two stations while no train stands at the first one: start at that station's stop connection (a train docked there starts its next route from it). A start equal to the goal gives a one-point route of 0 m, as `FindPathSync` does for a train standing at the station asked for. Cost on a 142-track save (i9-12900H, Shipping): `FindPathSync` 3.3 µs a call on average (longest 8.8), the search through `FGraphAStar` 11.6 µs (the first call 305 µs). This answers the **[source]** note on `FindPathSyncInternal` and the **[?]** on the cost of a route question. Evidence: `routes from locomotives: 35 asked, 28 found, 0 differ; the game 3.3 us a call (longest 8.8), the search from a connection 11.6 us (longest 304.5), 15.6 points a route`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **What the game's route graph looks like from code.** A node is a track connection, standing for "the far end of the track the train is on". `FRailroadGraphAStarHelper::GetNeighbourCount/GetNeighbour(Node)` give, for every connection `C` in `Node->GetConnections()`, the connection `C->GetOpposite()` (the far end of the next track); a Buffer Stop gives none. `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter::IsTraversalAllowed(Node, Neighbour)` is false exactly when the node's connection has a trailing signal and no facing signal (a one-way signal seen from behind). A walk that uses "leave a track through a connection unless it has a trailing signal and no facing signal, then enter every connected track" produced the same ways on as the game's two functions at all 284 track ends of a save with 142 tracks and 68 signals (block and path signals, one-way and two-way joints, switches with a signal on the point, a level crossing). Evidence: `ways on: 284 track ends, 238 ways, 0 ends differ from the game`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route between two stations asked while no train stands at the first one matches the route the game later gives a train there, also for a train that leaves backwards.** Asked through the public search from the first station's stop connection, and for a push-pull train from the far end of the track its rear locomotive would stand on (walk back from the stop connection by the train's length less half a vehicle; a locomotive-wagon-locomotive train at a station with two platforms has its rear locomotive two tracks behind the station's track), the answers were compared with `mAtcData.Path` of trains at the moment the game gave them their next route after docking: 25 departures of 7 trains on three networks (a one-way loop with a station bypass, a two-way line with a passing loop, a double-track network with a level junction), every point the same and the distance equal to `PathPoints[0].Distance`, including the 559.5 m route over the bypass around a station the train does not stop at. Evidence: `leaves <A> for <B> on the game's route of 14 points, 559.5 m: the search from the station gives the same route forwards`, `… route of 17 points, 585.1 m: the search from the station gives the same route backwards`, `25 drives between stations seen on 7 trains: 0 not within 1% or not the game's route`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **The distance a train will drive is the route's `Distance` plus the way from the locomotive's front to the route's first point, and that way can be negative.** A vehicle's `GetTrackPosition()` is its middle. For the leading locomotive the way ahead is `(Forward > 0 ? TrackLength - Offset : Offset) - GetLength() / 2`. A locomotive whose middle stood 1.1 m before the end of its track has its front 6.9 m into the next track, which the route counts in full: the game's route said 575.0 m and the train drove 568.0 m until it docked; with the negative way ahead added the prediction was 568.1 m. Clamping the way ahead at zero was the bug that showed it. For six other trains the prediction was within 0.1 m of the distance driven (126 to 773 m). Evidence: `reached its first stop after 568.0 m, the driver had said 575.0 m` before, `after 568.0 m, the driver had said 568.1 m` after. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- Cost of the game's own route search on a small layout (8 stations, 52 signals, 5 trains): a mod that asks about every pair of stations and every free place in its first cycle made 54–60 searches in the game's graph, which took 16–20 frames spread over the first cycle; later questions came from the mod's memory at about 20 µs each (36 587 answers from memory in a 34-minute run). Ask the game once per pair and remember the answer. Evidence: `routes: 54 searched in the game's graph, 36587 from memory, 20.2 us a search, longest 399.4 us`. (2026-10-06, game 502094, SML 3.12.0, UE 5.6.1-CSS)
- **A player's timetable route can be read leg by leg from the game's route search.** For each leg between two stops of a timetable, start the public route search (see "The game's route search can be run from any track connection") at the first stop's stop connection, as if the train were docked there. The tracks of the result's points (`Points[i]->GetTrack()`) are the tracks of the leg, and a point on a station's own track is a station the train passes without stopping. Compared with the tracks a train with the timetable B, A really drove, over four drives: 648.1 m read against 648.0–648.1 m driven, 559.5 m against 559.4 m, 32 tracks driven, 0 off the read route, 0 route tracks left undriven, and the station passed (`through <station C>`) named. Read again when the timetable, the tracks (a graph generation) or the consist changes. Evidence: `drive 1, <station B> to <station A>, 648 m, through <station C>: read 648.1 m, driven 648.0 m (-0.01%)`. (2026-10-06, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- The private `FindPathSyncInternal(start, end, filter, out_pathPoints)` is not needed for such routes (access via AccessTransformers would also give it). A route of your own can be given to the autopilot (see `domains/railroad/own-routes.md`, "Giving the autopilot a route of your own" below).
- **[source]** The edge cost is computed by `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter::GetTraversalCost`.
- **Passing through a station costs 100 m in the game's route search.** `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter::GetTraversalCost(A, B)` is the length in cm of the track that `B` ends, plus 10 000 when `B` is a station's stop connection: 11 600 for the 1 600 cm track of each of 11 stations, the plain length for all other 227 ways. This is the value behind "the game avoids a track through a station the train does not stop at": a bypass is taken when it is less than 100 m longer. The goal station's own stop connection carries the same surcharge, which changes nothing between routes to it. Evidence: `cost of T121.1 -> T122.1 is 11600.0 for 1600.0 cm of track, station <name>`, 11 such lines, `11 ways cost more or less than their length`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's pathfinding avoids a track through a station the train does not stop at.**
  - A 144.0 m track ran through a station (entry signal, three platform tracks, exit signal) and a 167.6 m bypass ran next to it, from a switch before the station to a merge behind it. Every train whose timetable did not name that station was routed over the bypass, although it is 23.6 m longer: the game's route between the two neighbouring stations was 559.5 m, against 536.0 m through the station before the bypass was built.
  - Where neither branch has a station the game takes the shorter one (128 m against 153 m, 228 m against 320 m on the same loop).
  - So passing through a foreign station costs more than 23.6 m of track in the route cost; the value is 100 m (next entry). A mod that predicts the game's route from track lengths alone gets this wrong; asking the game (`FindPathSync`) does not.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `the game itself routes … over the longer branch at T36.1 (167.6 m, the other branch 144.0 m)`, `route on …: the game's, 14 points, 559.5 m`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **`FindPathSync` searches only forwards from the locomotive it is given, and a station can be reached only in its own direction.** Asked for a parked push-pull train (locomotive, wagon, locomotive) at the end station of a two-way single-track line:
  - from the locomotive that faces the end of the track every other station was `RPFR_Unreachable` (0 points), from the locomotive at the other end the same stations were `RPFR_Success`. A mod that asks the game for a route has to ask from the locomotive that faces the way the train is to leave.
  - a station in the middle of the line that faces one way was `RPFR_Success` for the train coming from the side it faces and `RPFR_Unreachable` from both locomotives of the train at the other end: the pathfinder does not reverse a train on the way.
  - a train docks driving against the station building's forward vector: `GetActorForwardVector()` of the station points from the building towards its platforms, the side the train comes from.
  - Evidence: `route asked for vehicle 0 … to <far station>: RPFR_Unreachable, 0 points`, `route asked for vehicle 2 … to <far station>: RPFR_Success, 17 points, 576.0 m`, `route asked for vehicle 2 of <the train at the other end> to <middle station>: RPFR_Unreachable`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A heavier train accelerates more slowly, but the autopilot brakes it the same way.** This answers the **[source]** tutorial note on more than 4 wagons per locomotive. One locomotive with one Freight Car (330 t, 32 m) against one locomotive with seven Freight Cars (510 t, 128 m), both empty, two drives each on a double-track network, speed sampled every 0.1 s:

  | | L W, 330 t | L and 7 W, 510 t |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 → 30 km/h | 1.6–2.6 s | 3.4 s |
  | 0 → 60 km/h | 3.2–4.2 s | 5.9 s |
  | 0 → 90 km/h | 5.2–6.5 s | 9.4–9.5 s |
  | 60 km/h → standstill at a station | 11.7 s over 54–57 m | 10.9–11.1 s over 44–48 m |

  The heavier train needs about 1.5 times as long to reach a speed. Braking for a station stop follows a fixed profile to the stop point, whatever the mass. So travel times learned for one consist do not carry over to another, and the difference comes from starting, not from stopping. Evidence: `drive 2 of 4: <train> <station A> -> <station B>, route 540.8 m, driven 540.4 m in 37.2 s, top 93 km/h; 0 to 30/60/90 km/h in 3.4/5.9/9.4 s; 60 km/h to standstill in 10.9 s over 44.3 m`. (2026-10-06, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** `AFGTrainScheduler` (`FGTrainScheduler.h`): native block reservations, priorities and starvation protection. The game itself resolves conflicts at junctions. Tested through hooks: see `domains/railroad/signals.md`, "Holding a train at a signal with hooks" below.
- **[source]** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock` (`FGRailroadSignalBlock.h`): `GetAspectFor(signal)`, `IsOccupied`, `CreatePathReservation`, `CreateExclusiveReservation`. An ordinary block is exclusive. In a path block several trains run at once if their routes do not cross. Tested through hooks: see `domains/railroad/signals.md`, "Holding a train at a signal with hooks" below.
- **[source]** Aspects: `ERailroadSignalAspect` (none, clear, stop, docking).
- **[source]** Driving control: `UFGLocomotiveMovementComponent` has `SetThrottleInput`, `SetAirBrakeInput`, `SetEmergencyBrake`.

## What the autopilot does with a route

- **What the autopilot looks at: `mAtcData.TargetPoints`.** One point per signal on the route (with its current aspect), one per switch (aspect `RSA_None`) and one for the goal (`IsGoal`, aspect `RSA_Dock` when the goal is a station). Each point has `PathSegment` (the index of the route point) and `Distance` from the train.
  - `CurrentPathSegment` is the index of the route point at the far end of the track under the train's front. `CurrentConnection` is that point's connection.
  - A train stops 10.0 m before a signal at stop (`NextRestrictiveSignalStopOffset` 1000). The stop offset for docking is 8.0 m.
  - With no route (docked, or autopilot idle) the points have `PathSegment` -1 and list the signals along the track ahead.
  - Evidence (game 502094): control data logged at every tick in which it changed, for example `T24.0 segment 15 10 m … RSA_Stop`, `ahead T24.0 10.0 m, speed 0 km/h`, `restrictive T31.1 RSA_Dock … stop offset 8.0 m`.
- **When the game looks for a route by itself (trains running undisturbed).** `FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync` was called:
  - once for a train when its autopilot was switched on (on the next tick),
  - once after each docking ended: docking state `TDS_None`, then `TDS_ReadyToDock` one frame later, the call in the frame after that, then `TDS_None` again.
  - Nothing else: 11 calls for three trains in 190 s, although they followed each other from signal to signal.
  - Every call came on the game thread with the default filter (`AcceptsPartialSolution` 0, `IgnoreSignals` 0).
  - Evidence (game 502094): a test log with a hook on `FindPathSync` that only logs.
- **What makes the game look for a route again while a train is on its way.** Each action was done to a train cruising on its route with the autopilot on, and the `FindPathSync` calls for that train were counted for the next 8 s:
  - `AFGRailroadTimeTable::SetCurrentStop(<the same index>)`: no call, route kept.
  - `SetStops(<the same stops>)` (the current stop index stayed): no call, route kept.
  - `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(false)` followed by `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(true)` in one frame: the route was cleared and one call came at once, for a route from where the train was.
  - `SetCurrentStop(<another stop>)`: the route was cleared and one call came at once, to the new stop. The train kept rolling.
  - Dismantling a block signal on the route ahead of the train: no call in 44 s, route kept, no autopilot error.
  - Standing at a signal at stop: no call. `mSelfDrivingData.TimeWaitingAtSignal` counts the seconds. At 300 s (`mLongWaitAtSignalThreshold`, game 502094) the train's error becomes `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` and the status delegate fires with `TS_SelfDriving`. The game still does not look for another route (watched for 20 s after the error, on two trains). A train held at the exit signal of the station it just docked at keeps the docking state `TDS_ReadyToDock` while it waits.
  - So a mod that wants the game to ask for a route again (to answer in a `FindPathSync` hook) can toggle the autopilot or change the current stop. A mod that writes routes itself must expect the game to replace them after exactly these actions, after every docking and after every load of a save (see "A train's route is not saved" below).
  - Evidence (game 502094): `current stop changed to the other station: 1 pathfinding calls for the test train and 2 route changes on it in the 8 s after, error SDLE_NoError`.
