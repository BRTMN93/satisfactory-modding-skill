# Railways: what the autopilot does with a route

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Split from `domains/railroad/route-search.md`, which holds the route search, the route graph and the costs. Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain.

## What the autopilot does with a route

- **What the autopilot looks at: `mAtcData.TargetPoints`.** One point per signal on the route (with its current aspect), one per switch (aspect `RSA_None`) and one for the goal (`IsGoal`, aspect `RSA_Dock` when the goal is a station). Each point has `PathSegment` (the index of the route point) and `Distance` from the train.
  - `CurrentPathSegment` is the index of the route point at the far end of the track under the train's front. `CurrentConnection` is that point's connection.
  - A train stops 10.0 m before a signal at stop (`NextRestrictiveSignalStopOffset` 1000). The stop offset for docking is 8.0 m.
  - With no route (docked, or autopilot idle) the points have `PathSegment` -1 and list the signals along the track ahead.
  - Evidence (game 502094): control data logged at every tick in which it changed, for example `T24.0 segment 15 10 m … RSA_Stop`, `ahead T24.0 10.0 m, speed 0 km/h`, `restrictive T31.1 RSA_Dock … stop offset 8.0 m`.
- **When the game looks for a route by itself (trains running undisturbed).** `FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync` was called:
  - once for a train when its autopilot was switched on (on the next tick),
  - once after each docking ended: docking state `TDS_None`, then `TDS_ReadyToDock` one frame later, the call in the frame after that, then `TDS_None` again.
  - Nothing else: 11 calls for three trains in 190 s, although they followed each other from signal to signal.
  - Every call came on the game thread with the default filter (`AcceptsPartialSolution` 0, `IgnoreSignals` 0).
  - Evidence (game 502094): a test log with a hook on `FindPathSync` that only logs.
- **What makes the game look for a route again while a train is on its way.** Each action was done to a train cruising on its route with the autopilot on, and the `FindPathSync` calls for that train were counted for the next 8 s:
  - `AFGRailroadTimeTable::SetCurrentStop(<the same index>)`: no call, route kept.
  - `SetStops(<the same stops>)` (the current stop index stayed): no call, route kept.
  - `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(false)` followed by `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(true)` in one frame: the route was cleared and one call came at once, for a route from where the train was.
  - `SetCurrentStop(<another stop>)`: the route was cleared and one call came at once, to the new stop. The train kept rolling.
  - Dismantling a block signal on the route ahead of the train: no call in 44 s, route kept, no autopilot error.
  - Standing at a signal at stop: no call. `mSelfDrivingData.TimeWaitingAtSignal` counts the seconds. At 300 s (`mLongWaitAtSignalThreshold`, game 502094) the train's error becomes `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` and the status delegate fires with `TS_SelfDriving`. The game still does not look for another route (watched for 20 s after the error, on two trains). A train held at the exit signal of the station it just docked at keeps the docking state `TDS_ReadyToDock` while it waits.
  - So a mod that wants the game to ask for a route again (to answer in a `FindPathSync` hook) can toggle the autopilot or change the current stop. A mod that writes routes itself must expect the game to replace them after exactly these actions, after every docking and after every load of a save (see "A train's route is not saved" below).
  - The docs signal guide agrees that trains do not change their path after they have started moving; the measurement above is stronger (the route is cleared only by these two actions). (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0, docs.ficsit.app)
  - Evidence (game 502094): `current stop changed to the other station: 1 pathfinding calls for the test train and 2 route changes on it in the 8 s after, error SDLE_NoError`.
- When both ends of a push-pull train (a locomotive at each end) have a route to the next stop, the autopilot leads with the end whose route is shorter. Seen on a two-way line with a turning loop at one end: a train standing at a through station whose front faced the far end station had a 464.0 m route from its front and an 800.5 m route from its rear (round the loop); the game's route out of the station was the 13-point, 464.0 m one, driven by the front locomotive. The same search run from both ends of the standing train (`RdRoutes`-style `FGraphAStar` from the station's stop connection and from a connection one train length behind it) predicts the game's choice. Evidence: `the game leads the loaded train out of <station> FORWARDS: 13 points, 464.0 m (forwards 464.0 m, backwards 800.5 m)`, packaged game. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- Every point of the game's own route search (`FRailroadGraphAStarPathPoint::TrackConnection`) names its track through `GetTrack()`, and the points cover every track the train will drive over, so a property of your own track graph can be summed along a route: a mod listed the bidirectional track groups a drive passes through by walking the points and adding each track's group once, and the list came out in driving order (a drive over a two-way line with a passing loop between two end stations listed the start group and the end group, a drive to the intermediate station only the start group). Evidence: a test scenario's step line `route sections of <train>: <station A> -> <station B> reachable [308687557, 303491330], <station A> -> <station C> reachable [308687557]`. (2026-10-12, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's path finder turns a train with a locomotive at both ends only at a track end (a Buffer Stop or an open end), not at a station or a switch, and the route runs through whatever stands on the way.** On a single two-way line with a dead-end spur whose switch leads towards station A, a train L W W L standing at the far station B got a 1273 m route into the spur: along the whole line through A to the buffer stop behind it and back to the spur (the direct distance spur to B was 1265 m, to A 577 m). The route ignored the train standing at A: the moving train stopped at the path signal of A's block and waited there with `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` until the test timed out; with the other train moved to a branch station, it stopped at the block signal of the branch's junction block instead. A plain drive B to A and then into the spur from A's side (the train leaves A backwards) took 45 s plus 41 s. So a bidirectional train that must change direction to enter a siding needs a free track end beyond it, and a mod that takes the game's route should expect it to run through occupied stations. Evidence: four runs of a park-on-spur scenario, three failed and one passed. (2026-10-12, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Cost of the game's route search on a big save: 0.35 ms on average, up to 2.4 ms for one search.** A player's save with 1830 tracks, 398 signals, 19 stations and 10 trains: `FGraphAStar` searches between station stops (every pair of stations once after a load, 280–346 searches in 3 minutes) took 345–380 µs on average, the longest 2.0–2.4 ms, on four loads. One search cannot be split between frames, so a mod that searches after every load or track change has single frames of up to about 2.5 ms on such a network whatever its budget; it can only search less often or fewer pairs. On test layouts of 140 tracks the same searches took 17–39 µs. Evidence: `route searches 340, 324.5 us on average, most 2080.3 us`, `route searches 346, 348.1 us on average, most 2380.8 us`. (2026-10-13, game 502094)
- **A push-pull train longer than its station with a switch right behind the platforms: the walk back to the rear locomotive has to cross that switch, and the branch it takes decides the route.** The rear locomotive of a locomotive-wagon-locomotive train at a station with one platform stands beyond the switch at the platforms' end. Walking back from the stop connection by the train's length less half a vehicle and stopping at the switch gives a start one track too near (the route found then has an extra first point and the distance is short by the rest of the train). Following the single branch that leads on to another switch or to a station (a platform-owned track) and not the dead-end stub beside it gave the game's route exactly, both ways, on a line with the stub 97 m long behind one station: `leaves <A> for <B> on the game's route of 4 points, 208.0 m …: the search from the station gives the same route backwards`. Where both branches lead on, the train may have come over either, and the walk cannot know which. Also: the game's `PathPoints[0].Distance` leaves out the track under the leading locomotive (see above), and a train leaving backwards from beyond its station starts in the middle of a long track, so the distance it really drives is that value plus the way from the locomotive's front to the end of its track (79.1 m of 207.1 m in the case measured; the driven distance matched within 1%). A comparison against `PathPoints[0].Distance` alone reports a false 62% difference. Evidence: `<scenario>` two departures of one train, `the game's route of 4 points, 207.1 m (79.1 m of them to the end of the leading locomotive's track) … right`, `reached its first stop after 207.1 m, the driver had said 207.1 m: within 1%`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's A* takes a cost filter of your own, so a route with costs of your own (blocks other trains take) comes out of the game's own search over the game's own graph, no hook needed.** `FGraphAStar<FRailroadGraphAStarHelper>::FindPath(Start, Goal, Filter, Points)` accepts any type with the filter's functions (`GraphAStar.h`). A struct that holds a `FRailroadGraphAStarFilter`, forwards `GetHeuristicScale`, `GetHeuristicCost`, `IsTraversalAllowed` and `WantsPartialSolution` to it, returns true from `ShouldIncludeStartNodeInPath` (the game's filter does; without it point 0 is left out) and adds a penalty in `GetTraversalCost(From, To)` for the track that `To` ends (the far end of the next track, the track the edge drives) gives the game's exact routes while the penalties are zero: 20 ordered pairs of 5 stations searched from their stop connections and 15 locomotive routes against `FindPathSync`, 0 differ; 3.6 µs a search against 11.6 µs for the game's own filter on average (the game's first search of the session 419 µs). With 2000 m added to every block a standing train is in (the blocks of its vehicles' tracks, `GetSignalBlock()`; 500 m for a moving one) the route from the station before a 228 m main line with a standing train took the 320 m siding beside it (6 of 20 pairs changed). Evidence: `S9 cost: 20 pairs of stations over 3 rounds; the game's filter 11.6 us on average, most 418.8 us …; the mod's filter without penalties 3.6 us on average, most 8.7 us …, 0 differ; with live penalties (3 trains in 3 blocks) 3.6 us on average, …, 6 routes differ`, `locomotives: 15 routes asked, 12 found by the game, 0 differ from FindPathSync`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- A route from such a search written with `mAtcData.SetPath` in the same frame as the game's own route (the frame after the autopilot was switched on, the train still at 0 km/h; a core ticker runs after the game's pathfinding in that frame) was driven as it stood: the train took the 9 tracks of the siding, passed the siding's exit path signal without stopping (two stands of 0.6 s in the whole drive) and docked at its stop after 53 s. On the game's own route it stopped 10 m before the path signal at the switch before the occupied main line and waited there (62 s when the run ended, `TimeWaitingAtSignal` counting): a path signal needs the block behind the junction free, and the game's search does not route round an occupied block. Evidence: `S9 route written on <train> (as it sets off, through the siding): 27 points, 788.2 m, SetPath 1, segment 0 -> 0, at 0 km/h, frame 63 (0 frame(s) after the game's route)`, `docked at <station> after 53.2 s: 2 stands, the longest 0.6 s, … the mod's own tracks driven 9 of 9`; control run `next restrictive <path signal> RSA_Stop 10 m, … waiting at signal 62 s`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- The game clears `mAtcData.Path` when the train docks: a route the mod wrote was gone (`Path` null) in the frame the docking state became `TDS_Docked`, with the train at 0 km/h; this is not the "route dropped" case of a route that ends behind the train's front (`domains/railroad/own-routes.md`), so a mod that watches whose route a train has must expect a null path at docking. Evidence: `route cleared at the end on <train> at 0 km/h, docking TDS_Docked`. (2026-10-15, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A train's speed can be limited from code without a hook: write the rated maximum of every vehicle and recalculate the consist.** `UFGRailroadVehicleMovementComponent::mMaxVelocity` (km/h, a protected `EditAnywhere` UPROPERTY, written by reflection: `FindFProperty<FFloatProperty>(Movement->GetClass(), TEXT("mMaxVelocity"))`) on each vehicle of the train, then `AFGTrain::RecalculateConsistData()`: `mConsistData.MaxSpeed` and `MaxBrakeDistance` follow, and the autopilot's cruise target becomes the new maximum. A locomotive with a Freight Car (330 t, rated 200/200 km/h, driving at most 120 km/h) set to 40 km/h held 36.5-39.6 km/h over 533 m of each 640 m section (98-100% of the per-frame samples within -3/+2 km/h), kept it after the autopilot was switched off and on (a new route of the game) and after a 1 s docking at the station in the middle, and nothing put the values back (0 resets in 159 s). The autopilot approaches the target from below like a proportional controller: throttle 0.20 at 0 km/h, 0.01 at 39 km/h, 0.00 at 39.6 km/h. The braking for the next docking then starts about 18 m before its 29 m restricted zone (`MaxBrakeDistance` of 40 km/h = 18.5 m) with the air brake, and the train docked normally twice. The values are not saved (a load brings the class defaults back), so a mod writes them again after every load. Evidence: `<scenario> vehicle at 40 km/h: docked 2 of 2, autopilot errors 0, the game's routes 3, writes 0, put back by the game 0; section 1 to <station B>: held 40 km/h over 533 m for 49.1 s, 98.0% of 2945 samples within -3/+2 km/h, lowest 36.5, highest 39.6 ...; section 2 ... 100.0% of 2918 samples ... lowest 37.0, highest 39.6`. (2026-10-28, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **`mConsistData.MaxSpeed` alone is not enough: the braking curve towards a station or a signal is not clamped by it.** With `MaxSpeed` written to 40 km/h (cm/s) and nothing else, the train held 37-39.7 km/h on the open line (the game never put the value back, also not at a route change or a docking), but as soon as the dock point came within the consist's `MaxBrakeDistance` (458 m, still the value for the rated 200 km/h) the target jumped to 192.8 km/h, the braking curve of the dock point, and the train sprinted to 113-118 km/h before braking for the station. Evidence: `t=25.0 <train>: 39.1 km/h, target 40.0 ... restrictive RSA_None`, `t=26.0 <train>: 39.7 km/h, target 192.8 ... restrictive RSA_Dock 458 m`, section results `35.0% of 1595 samples within -3/+2 km/h, lowest 37.0, highest 113.3`. (2026-10-28, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **`FTrainAtcPoint::SpeedLimit` is dead data for the autopilot, and `mAtcData.TargetSpeed` is recomputed every tick.** The game leaves `SpeedLimit` at 0 on every target point, the dock goal included; 40 km/h written into every point from a core ticker every frame (69 writes) changed nothing (the target stayed 200 km/h, the train reached 120 km/h), and the game builds a fresh array with zeros whenever the train passes a point (54 of 56 values gone that way, 2 overwritten in place). `TargetSpeed` written every frame (4174 writes) changed nothing either: the throttle was 1.00 at 116.7 km/h with 40 in the field, so the game derives the throttle from the target it computed itself earlier in the tick. Evidence: `<scenario> points at 40 km/h: ... writes 69, points put back by the game 56 (54 of them in a fresh array), fresh arrays 16; ... highest 120.0`, `<scenario> target at 40 km/h: ... writes 4174 ... highest 120.3`. (2026-10-28, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **What the autopilot's target speed is, measured.** With nothing restricting: the consist's `MaxSpeed` (the rated maximum, 200 km/h for an Electric Locomotive with a Freight Car; the train drives at most about 120 km/h, by its tractive effort). A restrictive point (a station to dock at, a signal at stop) counts once it is within the consist's `MaxBrakeDistance` (463 m = 55.6² / (2 · 3.33) from the rated 200 km/h; seen at 459 m and 458 m, not at 515 m); from then on the target is the braking curve `sqrt(v_end² + 2 · a · (d − 29 m))` with `a` = `MaxBrakeDeceleration` (3.33 m/s² for that train) and `v_end` = 15 km/h for a docking (the restricted speed of the last 29 m before the stop point, stop offset 8 m): 94.8 km/h at 131 m, 131.2 km/h at 227 m, 185.8 km/h at 427 m. 0 to 120 km/h takes about 15 s and 250 m; 120 km/h to the dock about 10 s and 170 m with the air brake (`LastSpeedControl` -1). Evidence: trace lines `<train>: 118.3 km/h, target 185.8, brake 162 m ... restrictive RSA_Dock 427 m`, `99.0 km/h, target 94.8 ... air 1.00 ... RSA_Dock 131 m`, `14.5 km/h, target 15.0 ... RSA_Dock 13 m`. (2026-10-28, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A speed limit set through the vehicles' rated maximum is not saved, can be put back on at once after a load, and can be lifted on the way.** A train saved at 39.6 km/h with its vehicles' `mMaxVelocity` at 40 came back from the load (the same process, through the main menu) at 39.5 km/h with the class defaults again (`mMaxVelocity` 200/200, `mConsistData.MaxSpeed` 200 km/h) and the game's own route already found; writing the values again in the frame the train was found held it at the limit for the rest of the drive. In the first second after the load the game itself cut the speed from 39.5 to 33.4 km/h (`SDLE_NoPower` for 0.4 s, the same dip as 95 to 61 km/h in a load test without any limit), and the proportional control took 5 s to get back above 37 km/h and 15 s to 39.3. Lifting the limit on the way (the class defaults written back, `RecalculateConsistData()` at 39.5 km/h) let the train speed up to 113 km/h and dock normally; at 80 km/h the same mechanism held 77.0-79.5 km/h over 423 m. Evidence: `loaded <save>: <train> at 39.5 km/h (saved at 39.6), consist max 200.0 km/h, vehicles 200/200 km/h ... route yes`, `mMaxVelocity 200/200 -> 40/40 km/h written again after the load`, `the limit lifted at 39.5 km/h ... MaxSpeed 200.0 km/h`, `section 2 ... highest 113.2 ... docked 1`, `held 80 km/h over 423 m for 19.3 s, 100.0% of 1157 samples`. (2026-10-28, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **`mAtcData.NextSignalConnection` is the nearest signal within the autopilot's search distance, not the first signal on the train's route; to know which signal comes first, the route has to be walked.** `FTrainAtcData::UpdateNextSignal(searchDistance)` keeps "the closest signal within the search distance" (`FGTrain.h`). On a 650 m stretch with no signal between a train and the path signal at its end, a check through `NextSignalConnection` saw that path signal only about 400-460 m before it (train limited to 85-90 km/h), while a walk over `mAtcData.Path->PathPoints` from `max(CurrentPathSegment, 0)`, taking the first point whose `TrackConnection->GetFacingSignal()` is set, found it as soon as the train had passed the signal before it (about 600 m before it), in 2 of 2 runs each. An unlimited train (rated 200 km/h) saw a path signal 272 m ahead through `NextSignalConnection`. The game's path reservation does not wait for `NextSignalConnection` (see the reservation queue in `domains/railroad/scheduler.md`), so a mod's decision that "the path signal is next, the way is asked for" has to come from the route walk. Evidence: `Driver: train <train> does not yield to <other>: the junction's path signal is its next signal` logged with `<train>` about 460 m from the junction with the `NextSignalConnection` check and about 650 m with the route walk, same layout and start times. **[?]** The search distance seems to follow the consist's `MaxBrakeDistance` (a train limited to 40 km/h, 18.5 m, appeared to ask for its path only some tens of metres before its path signal); not measured directly. (2026-11-02, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The rated-maximum speed limit works on a dedicated server, and its clients see it without writing anything.** Written on the server (`mMaxVelocity` of every vehicle by reflection, then `AFGTrain::RecalculateConsistData()`), a limit of 40 km/h held 37.0-39.6 km/h over 532 m of each 640 m section (100% of the server's samples within -3/+2 km/h) and the train docked twice, as on a host. A game joined as a client measured the train from the replicated position of its leading vehicle: 1294 m driven, at most 39.9 km/h (the vehicle's replicated forward speed at most 39.6 km/h), with no write on the client. Evidence: server `<scenario> vehicle at 40 km/h: docked 2 of 2, autopilot errors 0 ...; section 1 to <station B>: held 40 km/h over 532 m for 48.7 s, 100.0% of 1045 samples within -3/+2 km/h, lowest 37.0, highest 39.6 ...`, client `client: <train> moved 1294 m, highest replicated speed 39.9 km/h (vehicle 39.6) against the limit 40 km/h, stood 20 s at the end`. (2026-10-28, game and dedicated server 513885, SML 3.12.0)
