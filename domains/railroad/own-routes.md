# Railways: own routes for the autopilot, push-pull trains and two-way single track

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

## Giving the autopilot a route of your own

- **The autopilot drives a route given by a mod, and signals follow that route.** A hook on `FindPathSync` (`references/sml-api.md`, "SML: hooks") replaced the result for one train with a route through a 320 m siding instead of the game's 228 m main line. The new route had the same first and last points as the game's route, with the siding's connections spliced in and `Distance` recomputed. It was a new `FRailroadPath` in `Result.Path` (`Goal` and `Path->Station` unchanged).
  - The train took the siding five times out of five, on a layout with block signals only and on one with path signals at the junction entrances. There was no autopilot error, and it docked normally.
  - The train kept the very `FRailroadPath` object the hook returned (`mAtcData.Path` compared equal to it) until the next docking.
  - The autopilot set the switch itself, and the path signal in front of the switch cleared for the mod's diverging route.
  - Other trains, whose results were left alone, kept to the main line.
  - Evidence (game 502094): two test runs, `autopilot errors 0`.
- **A route can be given to a train without any hook: `AFGTrain::mAtcData.SetPath(Result)`.** `mAtcData` is a public member and `FTrainAtcData::SetPath(const FRailroadPathFindingResult&)` is exported.
  - A ticker noticed a new route of the game on the train (`mAtcData.Path` was not the mod's object). It built a `FRailroadPathFindingResult` (`Locomotive`, `Goal` = the last point's connection, `Path` = a new `FRailroadPath` with the same `Station`, `Result = RPFR_Success`) and called `SetPath`.
  - `SetPath` returned true and `CurrentPathSegment` went back to 0. The train kept the mod's `FRailroadPath` object and drove it: three trips out of three through the siding, no autopilot error.
  - It works right at departure (train standing, the frame after the game's own `FindPathSync`) and on the way: at 14 km/h and at 104 km/h, three tracks into the route. The mod's route started at the far end of the track the train was on (the game's points from `CurrentPathSegment` on), the way the game's own routes start.
  - A route whose first point lies behind the train works too (see "A route can be changed under a moving train" below).
  - Evidence (game 502094): `route written into the control data on the way, at 104 km/h: SetPath 1, segment 3 -> 0, on the train now the mod's: 18 points, 568.1 m`.
  - **The game's own pathfinding can be noticed without a hook.** `AFGTrain::mSelfDrivingData.TimeOfLastFindPath` holds the world time (seconds) of the train's last pathfinding, and a new route is a new `FRailroadPath` object, so comparing `mAtcData.Path` with the object the mod wrote tells whose route the train has (this is what the ticker above compared). Evidence: `route on …: the game's, 14 points …, game's pathfinding clock 35.5` logged at t=35.5 s. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route can be changed under a moving train in both directions with `SetPath`.**
  - Made whole on the way: a route that ended at a signal was replaced by the whole route 100 m and 15 m before the stop point (at 56–91 km/h and at 13–16 km/h). The train did not stop.
  - Cut on the way: a whole route was cut at a signal ahead. The train stopped before that signal in every try: with 59 m and 9 m more room up to the stop point than `mAtcData.BrakeDistance` at that moment, and with 20 m, 60 m and 100 m less.
  - With room to spare, or 20 m short, it braked normally: from 111 km/h it stopped in 112 m and 6.3 s, 4.2–4.9 m/s² on average (`BrakeDistance` matches about 3.6 m/s², so it is on the safe side).
  - The end of a route is enforced, not only braked for. Cut 60 m and 100 m too late, the train still stood at the stop point, 8.0 m before the signal: from 76 km/h with 2.1 m of room and from 115 km/h with 41.9 m of room (about 12 m/s²). When the cut came with the front already 1.8 m past the stop point, the train stood still within one frame from 96 km/h, 5.7 m before the signal. No cut made a train pass the signal and none raised an autopilot error. So a late cut is safe and only looks unnatural; cut no later than `BrakeDistance` before the stop point to get a normal braking.
  - **A route that ends behind the train's front is dropped by the game at once.** This is the cut after the front has passed the signal.
    - `mAtcData.SetPath` was called with a route of two points, the track before a signal and the connection that signal guards, in the first tick after the train's front had passed that connection (0.1 m behind the front). It returned true and set `CurrentPathSegment` to 0; the control data then had no target points and target speed 0.
    - In the next tick the train had no route (`mAtcData.Path` empty), and one tick later it had a new route from the game's own pathfinding, from the track under its front to its timetable's current stop (`TimeOfLastFindPath` is that moment).
    - The train kept its speed (97 km/h, then 98 km/h; in the second try 27, then 28 km/h), raised no autopilot error and drove on; in the second try it docked 15 s later. Tried once behind a block signal and once behind a path signal.
    - So a cut that comes too late (tried once under each signal type, in the first tick after the front had passed the signal; **[?]** not with the whole train already behind it) does not stop the train and does not strand it in the junction. It only replaces the mod's route with the game's, and the train goes on to its timetable stop.
    - Evidence (game 502094): `route written … (cut at the parking signal, behind the front) at 97 km/h: SetPath 1, segment 5 -> 0, 2 points, 33.5 m`, `atc segment 0, ahead - 0.0 m, speed 97 km/h target 0 …`, `targets 0:`, `route on …: cleared, no path, at 97 km/h, tick 10319`, `route on …: the game's, 15 points, 520.0 m, … at 98 km/h, tick 10320, game's pathfinding clock 172.8`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
  - If the game had already approved the train's path reservation through the junction behind that signal (the path signal showed `RSA_Clear`), the signal went back to `RSA_Stop` after the cut: the game drops the reservation when the route changes.
  - A route may start behind the train: the same remaining route written with two extra points for the two tracks already passed was accepted (`SetPath` 1), `CurrentPathSegment` went to 0 and was at the right point again on the next ticks, and the train drove on.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `route cut 120.0 m before the parking signal at 111 km/h, braking distance 132.1 m, so -20.1 m to spare …: SetPath 1`, `stopped 8.0 m before parking signal … 8.3 s after its route was cut`, `route cut 6.2 m before the parking signal at 96 km/h, braking distance 98.5 m, so -100.4 m to spare before the stop point: SetPath 1`, `stopped 5.7 m before parking signal …, 2.0 s after its route was cut` (the scenario reports a stop after 2 s without movement), `route starting two tracks behind the train written at 30 km/h: SetPath 1, segment 2 -> 0`, `5 s after …: still the mod's 1, segment 5, 66 km/h`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A route given to the autopilot has to end at a connection the autopilot steers by: a signal or a station (**[?]** a switch). Otherwise the train stops at the last signal on the route.** The hook returned a route cut off at the connection guarded by a siding's exit path signal (last point = that connection, `Goal` = the same, `Path->Station` left as the real target).
  - The autopilot treated the last point as its goal: a target point with `IsGoal` and aspect `RSA_Dock`. It braked to a stop 8.0 m before that connection, like for docking.
  - The train then stood there in `SDLS_FollowPath`, docking state `TDS_None`, error `SDLE_NoError`, `TimeWaitingAtSignal` 0. The game made no further `FindPathSync` call for it for 90 s, while two other trains passed on the neighbouring track. The game does not look for a new route by itself in this state.
  - Switching the autopilot off and on makes it look: `FindPathSync` came on the next tick with a route starting where the train stood. The train then waited at the path signal (`TimeWaitingAtSignal` now counted up) and left when it cleared.
  - The same happened with a block signal in that place: the train stopped 8.0 m before the connection the signal guards and did not pass it, although nothing held the signal at stop.
  - So `FindPathSync` itself still takes a station, but the route handed to the autopilot can stop short.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `targets 1: T24.0 segment 18 8 m … RSA_Dock goal`, `held for 60 s: 0 pathfinding calls for the test train meanwhile, error SDLE_NoError, ai SDLS_FollowPath`.
  - A route written with `mAtcData.SetPath` that ended at a plain joint 56 m behind a block signal stopped the train 8.0 m before that signal, with one target point left: `targets 1: T14.0 segment 7 8 m RSA_Dock goal`. A route that ended at the end of a dead-end track (143 m behind the track's entry block signal), and one that ended one track earlier, stopped the train 8.0 m before that entry signal, four times out of four, standing across the switch in front of it.
  - **[source]** The cause is in `FTrainAtcData` (`FGTrain.h`): `TargetPoints` "does not contain a point that is only a pass-through (not relevant)", decided by the static `IsRelevantForATC(connection)`. The goal is put on the last relevant point of the route. Whether a switch connection without a signal works as a route's end was not tried.
  - The end of a track with a buffer stop is not such a point. The track's last connection is connected to a connection that has no track (`GetTrack()` null) and belongs to the buffer stop: its owner's class is `Build_RailroadEndStop_C` (an `AFGBuildableRailroadAttachment`, which has `IsEndStop()` and `GetConnection()`). A walk over the track graph has to skip such connections, and a dead end is a track end with no connection that has a track.
  - `FTrainAtcData::IsRelevantForATC(connection)` is public, static and exported, so a mod can ask which connections count. In a dump of one layout it was true for every connection with a facing signal, for the switch connections and for the stations' connections, and false for plain joints.
  - **A parking place on a dead-end track needs a signal inside the track, facing the entering train.** With a one-way block signal 111 m behind the track's entry signal and a route that ended at the connection it guards, the train stopped 8.0 m before that signal four times out of four, with all its vehicles inside the dead-end track's block (`targets 1: T04.1 segment 14 8 m RSA_Dock goal`). A second train passed the track's switch in both directions without stopping meanwhile. The parked train left backwards 0.6 s after it got a timetable and the autopilot. A station on the other side of the switch was unreachable from inside the track (`SDLE_StationUnreachable`): the way out leads only where the switch points.
  - In that layout a second one-way block signal stood 12 m further in, right at the buffer stop. It showed `RBV_NoExitSignals` (its block is the buffer stop's connection), while the parking signal before it was `RBV_Valid`. Without it the parking signal is not valid either: it gets `RBV_NoExitSignals` and `RSA_Stop` (`domains/railroad/signals.md`, "How signals are placed and what the game checks"). Evidence: dismantling the Buffer Stop signal from code turned the inner signal from `RBV_Valid` to `RBV_NoExitSignals` within 5 s. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
  - Evidence (game 502094): `… has stood for 8 s, 151.1 m before the end of its route T04.1 …, next signal T01.1 RSA_Dock 8 m … || targets 1: T01.1 segment 12 8 m RSA_Dock goal`, `T17.1 facing S15 atc at … to [Build_RailroadEndStop_C]`, `… has stood for 4 s, 8.0 m before the end of its route T04.1 …, vehicles: 0 locomotive on T04 block 13, 1 wagon on T04 block 13, 2 locomotive on T04 block 13`.
- **Parking a train at the end of a route, without touching the signals.** This extends "A route given to the autopilot has to end at a connection it steers by" above with a cycle that needs no hook, tested more than 40 times. A route written with `mAtcData.SetPath` that ends at the connection guarded by a siding's exit signal stops the train 8.0 m before that connection every time, with a path signal or a block signal there, also when the siding's entry signal is a path signal (the route then ends inside a path block).
  - The approach is like docking: from about 29 m before the end the target speed is 15 km/h, so the last 21 m take about 10 s.
  - No reservation is made behind the route's end. The path signal stays `RSA_Stop`, a block signal stays `RSA_Clear`, and trains on the neighbouring track passed the junction behind it without stopping (20 passes next to a held train, standstill 0.0 s, the same lowest speed as with no train held).
  - Held with the autopilot on (checked under a path signal only): nothing happens for 330 s. No pathfinding, `mSelfDrivingData.TimeWaitingAtSignal` stays 0 and `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` does not come (a train waiting at a signal gets it after 300 s, see above), because the train is not waiting at a signal. Writing the rest of the route with `SetPath` releases it: it moved 1.0 s later.
  - Parked with the autopilot off: `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(false)` after the stop gives `TS_Parked`, clears the route and leaves the train exactly in place (0.00 m in every hold, the longest 330 s). `SetStops` + `SetCurrentStop` + `SetSelfDrivingEnabled(true)` releases it: the game finds a route from where it stands on the next tick, and the train moves 0.6–1.0 s later when the signal ahead is clear, or waits at it like any train (up to 36 s seen) and then leaves.
  - Only the parked variant survives a save: see "A train's route is not saved" below.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `stopped 8.0 m before parking signal …, whole train in the siding's block 1, … waiting at signal 0 s`, `stood 330 s with the autopilot on: routes found by the game meanwhile 0, … waiting at signal 0 s, error SDLE_NoError`, `left siding … 13.9 s after the release, moving after 7.7 s`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A train's route is not saved; after a load the game finds a new one in the second tick.** `AFGTrain::mAtcData` has no `SaveGame` flag, and the test agrees: a train saved while it drove at 95 km/h on a route written by a mod came back with `mAtcData.Path` empty, the same position (0.43 m off) and the same speed in the first tick. In the second tick (0.4 s later) every train with the autopilot on had a new route from the game's own pathfinding, to its timetable's current stop. A mod that gave a train a route of its own has to write it again after every load; doing it in the tick the game's route appears worked (`SetPath` 1, the train took the mod's siding).
  - In that second tick every train with the autopilot on also reported `SDLE_NoPower` (with the No Power game rule on; **[?]** not tried on a save with power), cleared 0.2–0.3 s later. A mod that reacts to autopilot errors must ignore the first second after a load. The moving train lost speed in these frames: 95 km/h when loaded, 61 km/h in the second tick, 58 km/h in the third, then it accelerated again.
  - A train saved with the autopilot off came back parked in exactly the same place (0.00 m off), status `TS_Parked`, no route, and stayed there.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `loaded …: train …, autopilot 1, … speed 95 km/h (saved at 95), 0.43 m from where it was saved, route no path`, `[frame 1] t=0.4 event … error SDLE_NoPower`, `[frame 1] route on …: the game's, 14 points … at 61 km/h, tick 2`, `[frame 3] t=0.7 event … error SDLE_NoError`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** `FTrainAtcData::ClearPath()` (`FGTrain.h:174`) is public and drops the train's current route; a mod that ends a route of its own can call it so the game finds its own route again (in the second tick, as after a load). Its effect in the packaged game is not observed yet. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A route to a signal written with `SetPath` while the train rolls is accepted the same way as one written while it stands: after the autopilot was switched off and on at 29 km/h, the game found its own route in the next tick, the mod wrote its route again (`route … written (the game replaced the route): 14 points, 488.1 m, at 29 km/h, SetPath 1`) and the train stopped 8.4 m before the signal. A mod route that branches off the game's route must be compared point by point with the game's route (the common prefix must end before the game's route does): the switch's own curve track lies between the game's route and the siding's signal block, so a check by tracks ("every track of the way is on the route or in the siding's block") refuses a correct cut. Evidence: with the track check no cut happened on three saves (`cannot stop at … any more`), with the prefix check `route of train … cut at <signal>, 413 m before the stop point at 26 km/h, braking distance 10 m` and the train stopped there. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A train that was moving when the game was saved comes back moving after the load; with its autopilot switched off in the mod's first frame it kept rolling (29 km/h at the load, 25 km/h when the autopilot went on again a few seconds later) instead of braking. **[?]** Whether it would brake to a stop on its own with the autopilot off for longer was not watched. Evidence: `after the load: <train> at 29 km/h, autopilot 0, run yes, phase 3, graph 0`, then `route of train … written (the game found its route): 10 points, 375.3 m, at 25 km/h`. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)

## Hooking the route search

- `FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync(AFGLocomotive*, AFGBuildableRailroadStation*, FRailroadGraphAStarFilter)`: a static member function that returns a struct by value. A "before" hook on it works:
  `SUBSCRIBE_METHOD(FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync, [](auto& Scope, AFGLocomotive* Locomotive, AFGBuildableRailroadStation* Station, FRailroadGraphAStarFilter Filter) { … })`
  - There is no `Self` argument.
  - `Scope(Locomotive, Station, Filter)` runs the original and returns its result. `Scope.Override(Result)` replaces it. A handler that returns without touching `Scope` lets the original run.
  - Log line: `LogNativeHookManager: Display: Successfully hooked function FRailroadNavigation::FindPathSync at 00007FFD5FAD6D90`.
  - A train drove the route given through `Override` (`domains/railroad/own-routes.md`, "Giving the autopilot a route of your own").

## Push-pull trains and two-way single-track lines

- **Push-pull trains (a locomotive at each end) on a two-way single-track line: timetable, docking, own routes and parking work as on a loop.**
  - Two such trains ran five round trips each between two end stations that face opposite ways, passing each other on a one-way passing loop: 20 dockings, no autopilot error, 0.4 s of standstill per leg, 576 m in 37–43 s.
  - Docking: `TDS_ReadyToDock` and `TDS_Docked` in one tick. The leading locomotive stands at the station building, the wagon on the first platform, the rear locomotive on the second (an empty platform). When the docking ends, the game finds the next route from the locomotive at the other end: its first point is the far end of the track under that locomotive. Nothing has to be done to reverse the train.
  - A train also leaves backwards from plain track: parked with the autopilot off in front of a signal, then given a timetable stop behind it and the autopilot, it moved 0.6 s later on a route from its rear locomotive (five times out of five).
  - `mAtcData.SetPath` works on two-way track: a route cut at the exit path signal of a loop track stopped the train 8.0 m before it, five times, with the other train passing on the other loop track without stopping.
  - `AFGTrain::mSimulationData.FirstVehicle` is not the vehicle that leads in the driving direction: it stayed on the same vehicle while the train drove with the other end first.
  - **The docking time of a stop that moves no cargo is its `DockingRuleSet.DockForDuration`.** With the none descriptor as load and unload filter and the default docking definition, a stop took 15 s (the default duration) and 1.0–1.1 s with `DockForDuration = 1.f`. Test timetables that only need the stop to happen can use that to save 14 s per stop. The same-frame docking end with the none filter as the only load filter (the "nothing" filter, above) was a different setting; why the two differ was not investigated.
  - A timetable stop the pathfinder cannot reach gives the error `SDLE_StationUnreachable` at once; the train stands with `SDLS_Idle` and no route, and the error clears when the autopilot is switched off.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `finished: 1 steps, autopilot errors 0`, `docked at … vehicles: 0 locomotive on T21 …, 1 wagon on T22 …, 2 locomotive on T23`, `stopped 8.0 m before parking signal S19 (RSA_Stop)`, `event … error SDLE_StationUnreachable`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **The locomotives a route can start from have a free front coupler.** `!Locomotive->IsCoupledAt(ERailroadVehicleCoupler::RVC_FRONT)` picked, on 8 trains (locomotive + wagons, two locomotives + wagons, locomotive-wagon-locomotive), a set of at most two locomotives that reached every station any locomotive of the train reached with `FindPathSync`: the head locomotive, and the rear one of a push-pull train (`IsOrientationReversed()` 1, track position `Forward` -1). The second of two locomotives at the head has its front coupled and is left out. So a mod need not ask from every locomotive of a long train. Evidence: `vehicle 2 locomotive … forward -1, reversed 1, coupled front 0 back 1, can lead`, `vehicle 1 locomotive … coupled front 1 back 1` (not leading), 0 trains whose leading locomotives reached fewer stations than all its locomotives. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's signalling alone does not protect a two-way single-track stretch that is split into several blocks.** On a line where the stretch between a passing loop and an end station was split by the signals of a switch, and the station was a block of its own:
  - a train that followed another one to the end station drove up to the station's entry signal and stopped there; the first train, done with docking, could not leave because the block in front of the station was taken. Both stood at red signals with `TimeWaitingAtSignal` counting and no autopilot error (watched for 60 s; the long-wait error comes at 300 s).
  - a train that followed a train parked on a one-way loop track stopped 10 m before the path signal in front of the loop's switch, on the two-way track, and so closed the line for both directions.
  - **[source]** The rule players give for this: every two-way stretch between two passing places is one undivided block, the end station included, or its entry is a path signal chained up to the station (**[?]** this sits badly with "no path signal directly before a station" above; the signal kind at a station entry was not tested) (Steam discussions; signal guide on docs.ficsit.app). Not tested here.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `head-on standstill, both trains have stood for 60 s with the autopilot on: … waiting at signal 56 s, next signal T21.0 RSA_Stop 8 m … || … waiting at signal 62 s, next signal T03.1 RSA_Stop 10 m`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
