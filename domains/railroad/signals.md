# Railways: signals, track connections, holding a train at a signal

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

## Signals and track connections

- **A signal between two tracks, seen from the connections.** Take a one-way block signal at the joint between track A (before the signal) and track B (behind it):
  - `GetGuardedConnections()` is A's connection at the joint. `GetObservedConnections()` is B's connection at the joint.
  - A's connection returns the signal from `GetFacingSignal()`. B's connection returns it from `GetTrailingSignal()`.
  - `GetObservedBlock()` is B's block (`AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()` of B has the same `ID`).
  - A connection with a trailing signal and no facing signal cannot be entered from that side. This is how a walk over the track graph sees a one-way signal from behind.
  - Evidence: a dump of 12 block signals and 37 tracks, for example `signal S03 … guards [T10.1], observes [T09.0], observed block 3`.
- **Signal block numbers are per track network.** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::ID` (through `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()`) starts again in every connected track network (`GetTrackGraphID()`): a save with four networks had a block 12 in three of them. Two tracks share a block only when both the block number and the track graph id are equal. Evidence: a dump listing tracks of networks 2, 11 and 15 all under `block 12`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Track graph IDs (`GetTrackGraphID`) change when networks are joined or split; read them again each cycle instead of storing them per station.** A track joining two networks gave the second network the first one's ID (36 to 31); dismantling it gave one half a new ID. Evidence: `<scenario> SPLIT 2 s after the dismantle: graph IDs 1 1 1 1 gone 31 31 31 31`. (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Tracks that cross without a joint are put into one signal block, and `GetOverlappingTracks()` names them.** On a double-track line a branch left one track through a switch and crossed the other track on the level. The crossed piece of the other track, fenced by its own entry path signal and exit block signal, had the same block number as the switch's tracks, the path signals of both tracks observed that one block (`IsPathBlock()`), and all signals were `RBV_Valid`. `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetOverlappingTracks()` of the crossed piece returned the branch's curve and the other way round. The two branches of every switch are listed as overlapping each other too (they lie close behind the point), which changes nothing because they share the switch's block anyway. Two parallel tracks closer than 6 m (`RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE`) share a block as well, see the next entry. Evidence: `track T025 block 6 overlaps [T027 block 6]`, T025 joined only to its own line; `signal S50 path … observes [T025.0], observed block 6 (path block)`, `signal S51 path … observes [T028.0], observed block 6 (path block)`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Parallel tracks 5 m apart share a block, 6 m apart do not (closes the [?] on `RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE`).** A Railway piece laid parallel to a straight track, not joined to it, its middle 5.00 m from the other track: the game listed the two as overlapping (`GetOverlappingTracks()`), gave both the same block number, and put the loose piece into the other track's graph (`GetTrackGraphID()`). Pairs of tracks without a common joint whose closest points lay 5.97 m and 6.56 m apart (beside switches) had different blocks. Distances measured by sampling both splines every 2 m (`GetWorldLocationAndDirectionAtPosition`), so up to about 0.2 m long. A siding check that reads the game's blocks needs no distance rule of its own. Evidence: `new track …: 56.00 m … net 0, block 13, the game lists as overlapping [… net 0 block 13, 5.00 m from the new track's middle]`; `… 5.97 m apart, blocks 12 and 13 (apart)`. (2026-10-13, game 502094)
- **Path signals as a train sees them.** A path signal shows `RSA_Stop` until an approaching train gets its reservation, so the autopilot brakes for it.
  - A train doing 100 km/h slowed to 51 km/h. The signal turned `RSA_Clear` when the train was 48 m in front of it, and the train accelerated again (same numbers on two laps).
  - At a path signal whose block was busy, the train stood 10.0 m in front of it and left when the signal cleared.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `T34.1 segment 7 48 m … RSA_Stop` and `… RSA_Clear` in the same tick.
- **Block signals and path signals open the same window.** `mInteractWidgetSoftClass` read from instances of `Build_RailroadBlockSignal_C` and `Build_RailroadPathSignal_C` is the same class, `/Game/FactoryGame/Buildable/Factory/Train/Signal/Widget_RailroadSignal.Widget_RailroadSignal_C`. So one widget hook on that class covers both kinds (hooks: `references/sml-api.md`, "SML: hooks"; the window's widget tree: `domains/railroad/ui-windows.md`). A path signal with no train near it shows `RSA_Stop` with `RBV_Valid`. Evidence: a dump of 9 block and 3 path signals.
- **After a save is loaded, the signals on the track connections are known in the first tick; the signal blocks and the trains' graph id are not.**
  - In the first tick every signal's guarded connection already returned that signal from `GetFacingSignal()` (and the observed one from `GetTrailingSignal()`), so a walk over tracks, switches and one-way signals works at once. A scenario found both sidings of a 46-track loop in its first tick, while all signals still showed `RSA_Stop` without a block.
  - In the first frame of the loaded world every signal reported `HasObservedBlock() == false`, `GetAspect() == RSA_Stop` and `GetBlockValidation() == RBV_Unvalidated`. `AFGTrain::GetTrackGraphID()` returned -1, while the track under the train's first vehicle already returned its graph id.
  - The block signals turned `RSA_Clear` one frame later (0.4 s on that load), so the wait for the blocks is about one frame, not two seconds. The first test saw all 12 signals with a block and `RBV_Valid`, and the trains returning the graph id, at frame 62 (about two seconds) without looking more finely; when exactly the trains' graph id comes was not measured.
  - Code that only needs the layout does not have to wait. Code that needs block ids or aspects waits for `HasObservedBlock()`, for example until every signal with `HasValidConnections()` has it. In the first frames it should take the graph id from a track, not from the train.
  - Evidence (game 502094): test log, frame 0: `12 of 12 signals observe no block yet`, `track graph -1, its first vehicle's track is in graph 2`. Frame 62: all valid. Second test: `[frame 0] t=0.0 sidings read 0.00 s after the load, in tick 1`, `[frame 0] signal S07 shows RSA_Stop`, `[frame 1] t=0.4 signal S07 shows RSA_Clear`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Replacing signals changed the track graph.** A player replaced three block signals with path signals, and the track graph came back with 38 tracks instead of 37. One 56.43 m track had become two, with the same summed length, and the new joint carried one of the new signals (game 502094).
  - A signal placed in the middle of a track piece makes the game split that piece into two track buildables (a players' tutorial for 1.0 says free placement "creates new segments"): a player-built signal removed a 48.00 m track actor and two new ones of 32.39 m and 15.61 m appeared, each announced by the buildable delegates (`references/game-classes.md`, "mBuildableRemovedDelegate"). Evidence: build events `gone [… 48.00 m]`, `new tracks [… 32.39 m …; … 15.61 m …]`, and 0 changes without a build event. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
  - So a mod's own track graph and any stored reference to a track must be refreshed when a signal is built, not only when tracks are.
- `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetIsOwnedByPlatform()` tells a platform's own track piece from the rest, and the station building's track is found through the connection's `GetStation()`; together they find the tracks a train stands on in a station (both ends of a docked train). Dismantling a signal from code (`IFGDismantleInterface::Execute_Dismantle(Signal)`) merges its two blocks: a mod graph rebuilt 2 s after the build event saw the block of the signal's track containing the tracks beyond the gone signal (packaged game, 2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0).
- Dismantling one signal of a pair that stands on both sides of a joint leaves the other one as a one-way signal: a train that would have to pass it from behind gets `ESelfDrivingLocomotiveError::SDLE_StationUnreachableWithSignals` as soon as its autopilot asks for the route, and the leg ends with no route. Evidence: a path signal dismantled from code (`IFGDismantleInterface::Execute_Dismantle`) with its block signal partner kept, `ends: ESelfDrivingLocomotiveError::SDLE_StationUnreachableWithSignals` 5 s after the leg began. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A signal's lamps are not a mesh of their own.** `AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::mSignalComponent` (protected, a `UFGColoredInstanceMeshProxy`) shows one static mesh with one section and one material (path signal: `SM_TrainSignalType02_01` right-handed, `_02` left-handed, material `MI_TrainSignalType_2`; the block signal's Blueprint names `SM_TrainSignalType01_01`/`_02` **[source]**), and the lamps' colours come from the material through the signal's visual state (custom data), so a lamp cannot be shown, hidden or recoloured apart from the rest of the signal. The mesh has no CPU access (`bAllowCPUAccess` 0), so its triangles cannot be read either. The path signal's mesh box is X −75…360, Y ±112, Z −59…1104 cm (about 11 m high), the component sits at relative location (0, 450, 0) with yaw −90. Evidence: a dump of 6 path signals' mesh components on a test save. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A signal's head can be found from its mesh's collision boxes, in the cooked game.** `GetStaticMesh()->GetBodySetup()->AggGeom.BoxElems` of the signal's mesh component gives unrotated boxes in the mesh's space; the box that reaches highest is the top of the head, and line traces against the signal alone (`AActor::ActorLineTraceSingle`, `ECC_Visibility`) hit the same boxes. Measured, mesh space in cm: Block Signal head x −59.3…169.1, y ±19.2, top 1113.8 (lamps look along Y); Path Signal boxes near the top y ±58.3 together, top 1085–1095. A component added to the signal (a `UStaticMeshComponent` with `/Engine/BasicShapes/Cube`, attached to the signal's mesh component, `RF_Transient`, no collision) stays out of the save and shows on every machine that makes it. Evidence: three cubes turned 0/60/120° about one axis drawn as a hexagonal prism on both signals, three parts after switching on, none after switching off, after a save and a load again three. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's signal lamp material is in the cooked game and takes a colour.** `/Game/FactoryGame/Buildable/Factory/Train/Signal/MI_TrainSignal_Green` loads with `LoadObject<UMaterialInterface>`; its one vector parameter is `Color`, and a `UMaterialInstanceDynamic` with `Color` (0, 0, 1) shows a glowing light blue. Evidence: the log line of the material and screenshots of the shape on two signals. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The signal lamp material glows, and its `Color` is an emissive value: the game's lamps use about 1.** `MI_TrainSignal_Green` has `Color` (0.0107, 1, 0), `MI_TrainSignal_Red` (1, 0.015, 0) (`GetVectorParameterValue`). A copy with another colour at a brightest channel of 1 or more turns almost white on screen by day (pure blue at 4.3 looked almost white), at 0.1–0.2 it keeps its hue (blue 0.1 showed #0000C8, violet #8000FF 0.2 showed #A938E5). At night the eye adaptation brightens everything, the game's own red and green lamps turn pale too, and the same blue 0.1 showed #3C76F1, violet 0.2 #DABDFC. The exposure adapts over seconds, so a screenshot is judged about 15 s after changing the time of day (`AFGTimeOfDaySubsystem::SetDaySeconds`). Evidence: screenshots of a mark in this material on a <Path Signal> and a <Block Signal> at several values by day and at midnight, colour sampled at the mark's centre. (2026-10-23, game CL 513885)
- `AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::mIsLeftHanded` (`UPROPERTY(SaveGame, Replicated)`, read with `IsLeftHanded()`) is enough to make a signal left-handed for a test: it is set by reflection (`FindFProperty<FBoolProperty>(AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::StaticClass(), TEXT("mIsLeftHanded"))`) on the signals of <scenario>, the save is stored under a new name, and after loading that save the game builds the left-hand meshes itself. Evidence: after the load the log shows the meshes `SM_TrainSignalType01_02` (Block Signal) and `SM_TrainSignalType02_02` (Path Signal) and `IsLeftHanded()` 1 on the changed signals; the right-hand ones keep `..._01`. Setting the field in a running world without a save and load does not swap the mesh **[?]** (not tried). (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** `GetGuardedConnections()` returns `TArray<UFGRailroadTrackConnectionComponent*>` by value (a copy of `mGuardedConnections`, `FGBuildableRailroadSignal.h:75`). A range-for over the call is fine, but `const auto& List = Signal->GetGuardedConnections();` keeps only a temporary alive and a pointer into it dangles once the statement ends; in hot code it is called once per signal, because it allocates each time. (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Signal side and direction from the build-gun hologram:** the side (left/right of the track) is the hologram's build mode, the one R cycles: `SetBuildModeOverride(<mode with "Left" or "Right" in its class name>)` + `OnBuildModeChanged(mode)`. The direction the signal faces (which trains it guards) is the mouse wheel: `Hologram->ScrollRotate(1, Hologram->GetRotationStep())`, then place again. The user pointed this out; built that way, block and path signals came out on the wanted side and, after one scroll where needed, facing the wanted trains (checked by the guarded connection's normal against the trains' way). Evidence: <scenario>, `signal <X> (path, left): path, left, faces the trains it should` after `dismantled, built again (try 1: turned …)`. (2026-10-25, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)

## How signals are placed and what the game checks

Rules for build lists, test layouts and layout checks. Collected from the game headers, the wiki and the signal guide hosted on the modding docs; the items with evidence were also read from a built layout with 22 signals, all `RBV_Valid`.

- **A signal is directional. One signal at a joint makes the joint one-way; a two-way track needs a pair at the same joint, one per direction.** In code: a signal that has a partner at its joint returns `IsBiDirectional() == true` and each of the two connections has one signal as `GetFacingSignal()` and the other as `GetTrailingSignal()`. A lone signal returns false, and the connection behind it has only a trailing signal, so nothing may pass it from that side. Evidence: `signal S07 block …, two-way 0, guards [T14.0]`, `T15.1 trailing S07`; `signal S13 block …, two-way 1`, `T18.0 facing S13 trailing S14`. Wiki: "If only one signal is present, it will prevent trains in the opposite direction from passing through." (https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Train_Signals)
- **A signal placed at a switch sits on the switch's point and covers both branches**: it guards or observes two connections. Evidence: `signal S08 block …, guards [T14.1 T16.0], observes [T10.1]` and its partner `signal S21 path …, guards [T10.1], observes [T14.1 T16.0], observed block 4 (path block)`.
- **A block entered through path signals is a path block**: `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::IsPathBlock()` was true for exactly the blocks whose entry signals were path signals. **[source]** All entry signals of a block must be of one kind, exit signals may differ (wiki).
- **[source]** The block validation errors, enum `ERailroadBlockValidation` in `FGRailroadSignalBlock.h`, with the text the player sees (wiki): `RBV_NoExitSignals` "Block has no exit signal", `RBV_ContainsLoop` "Signal loops into itself" (the signal does not divide anything, also when two unconnected tracks lie too close: **[source]** the headers (`RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE = 600.f`, cm, `Buildables/FGBuildableRailroadTrack.h:371`) and a tutorial give 6 m, confirmed by the 5 m / 6 m measurement above; the docs signal guide advises keeping parallel tracks about 1.5 Foundations (about 12 m) apart, which is a safety margin above the 6 m threshold; unconnected parallel tracks 6, 7, 8, 12, 16 and 24 m apart had separate blocks, 5 m apart `RBV_ContainsLoop`: `<scenario> SIGNAL ... RBV_Valid, ... nearest unconnected track 6.00 m away, block -1 (-)` (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)), `RBV_ContainsMixedEntrySignals` "Block has conflicting entry signal types", `RBV_ContainsStation` "Path block cannot contain stations". A signal at the very end of a track has no valid connections ("Signal has missing connections", `HasValidConnections()` false). A signal with an error counts as red.
- **[source]** A path signal is red until an approaching train has its path reserved; the reservation runs through every following path signal up to the next block signal, and the block behind that signal must be free ("trains will only enter a Path Segment if the following Block Segment is clear", signal guide on docs.ficsit.app). A train does not stop inside a path block. So: path signal on every entrance of a junction, block signal on every exit, and no path signal directly before a station (the station would be inside the path block). Players also put block signals on the stretches between junctions, so more than one train fits on a line: a layout built that way by a player had 22 signals, all valid, and two shuttle trains ran 5 courses each without an error (2026-10-04, game 502094, SML 3.12.0). Claims of the wiki and the community signal guide, read through a summary, tested (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0): a train driven from the cab occupies its block, and a path signal ahead stays `RSA_Stop` for it (the game does not stop a driven train; it ran past the red signal: `<scenario> POSSESS t=17.6 ... own block 0 occupied 1 by it 1, path signal RSA_Stop -22.2 m ahead`). Wrong: a light train (locomotive and two empty Freight Cars) at 120 km/h began braking for a red block signal 152 m ahead, not about 250 m (`braking for <signal> began 152 m before it, from 33.1 m/s`; heavier trains not measured); signals 1 m and 4 m from a free track end have valid connections (`connections guarded 1 observed 1 (valid 1)`), so the signal logic has no 12 m minimum (**[?]** whether the signal hologram refuses such spots was not tried). **[?]** Not tested: a path block should be level or trains may collide (needs an over-crossing inside one path block). Advice, not a game rule: block spacing of 3–4 track lengths at most (wiki) or about 1.5 train lengths (guide); blocks of any length worked. The side of the track a signal stands on (wiki: Left Side/Right Side build modes since 1.1) does not matter for trains: docs.ficsit.app says "trains only look for signals on the right-hand side", which a test disproved (entry below); in the headers handedness only picks the mesh and transform (`mRightHandedMesh`/`mLeftHandedMesh` with their transforms, `Buildables/FGBuildableRailroadSignal.h:19-29`; `IsLeftHanded()` :110-112; `mIsLeftHanded` :291-293; hologram build mode `mBuildModeLeftHanded`, `Hologram/FGRailroadSignalHologram.h:63`), and the logic binds a signal to track connections as facing/guarded and trailing/observed (`FGBuildableRailroadSignal.h:215-235`). So the docs claim is outdated (older left/right build modes) **[source]**. Docs agree with the **[source]** text above on: no path signal directly before a station (`RBV_ContainsStation`, `FGRailroadSignalBlock.h:19`) and chained path signals reaching the next block signal. (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0, docs.ficsit.app)
- **[source]** The game's pathfinding ignores other trains and takes the shortest route, so two two-way tracks side by side are no passing loop: both directions pick the same one. A passing loop needs each of its two tracks made one-way, with a single block signal behind the switch where a train enters the loop track and a single path signal before the switch where it leaves it (Steam discussion 6982351509012486495; the built layout above is signalled this way). The docs signal guide says the same ("a pass around", the shortest path decides). (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0, docs.ficsit.app)
- **The signal's side does not change its logic (the docs' "trains only look for signals on the right-hand side" is wrong).** A Block Signal and a Path Signal built Left Side behave like Right Side ones at the same spot: on a one-way loop a train held by a train in the block behind stopped with its front 2.00 m before the signal (`RSA_Stop`) and moved 6.1 s (block) and 1.5 s (path) after it turned `RSA_Clear`, the same on both saves, 2 runs each; `RBV_Valid`, 0 invalid signals. The side only shows in `IsLeftHanded()`. Evidence: `<scenario> stop A front 2.00 m before it, aspect RSA_Stop, left-handed 1` against the same line with `left-handed 0`. (2026-10-25, game 513885, SML 3.12.0) The same holds at switches and in a two-way pair: a loop with its six switch and siding signals built Left Side ran two trains 600 s with the same 18/18 arrivals and 0 invalid signals as the all Right Side save (`<scenario> layout: 0 signals not valid, 13 non-path blocks; route ... signals: path L , block L , block R , ...`), and a two-way stretch with Left Side entry signals and Right Side opposite numbers ran two push-pull trains 14 stops each, never both in one block. (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A partly signalled network works; "signals are all or nothing" (community signal guide) is advice, not a game rule.** A one-way loop with a passing siding and four stations, once with all 13 signals and once with only the 7 of one straight (the other straight with both switches, the siding and its station one block): two trains on the game's timetables, 600 s, 2 runs each: 0 invalid signals, `SDLE_NoError`, no deadlock, never both trains in one non-path block. The only cost was capacity: one train arrived 14 times instead of 18. Evidence: `<scenario> t=600 arrivals A-train 14 D-train 21, … both in one block 0 times 0.0 s, errors SDLE_NoError / SDLE_NoError` (7 signals) against `18 / 18` (13 signals). The waiting counter read 0 s because the trains waited docked at stations. (2026-10-25, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
  - The 21 arrivals are real, not a stop counted twice: counted once per station change, the D-train calls at three stations per lap and the A-train at two, both laps about 90 s. The same loop with no signals at all ran two trains 600 s, 18/18, no error (`<scenario> t=600 arrivals A-train 18 D-train 18 (once per station: 18 / 18), ... errors SDLE_NoError / SDLE_NoError`); those trains never caught up with each other, so this does not show that an unsignalled network protects trains that meet. A two-way stretch and its end station left undivided worked (`domains/railroad/push-pull-lines.md`). A signalled area ending in a Buffer Stop with no signal in front of it does not work: its last block signal reports `RBV_NoExitSignals` and stops every train; one more block signal facing the Buffer Stop fixed it (the dead-end entry below). (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** The modding docs have no page on the signal classes; their only railway text is the community signal guide (https://docs.ficsit.app/satisfactory-modding/latest/CommunityResources/TrainSignalGuide.html). The FicsIt-Networks reflection pages list `RailroadSignal` and `RailroadSignalBlock`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A signal deep in a dead-end siding, facing the train that drives in, needs a second signal between it and the Buffer Stop or the game marks it `RBV_NoExitSignals` ("Block has no exit signal") and shows `RSA_Stop`: the stub it observes has no exit. With the second signal at the Buffer Stop the inner signal is `RBV_Valid` and the second one carries the error instead. A train whose route ends at the inner signal stops there either way (the route's end, not the aspect, stops it). This answers the **[?]** in `domains/railroad/own-routes.md` ("Whether the parking signal would be valid without it was not tried"): it is not. Evidence: dismantling the Buffer Stop signal from code turned the inner signal from `RBV_Valid` to `RBV_NoExitSignals, aspect RSA_Stop` within 5 s (packaged game, 2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0).
- On a two-way line with a passing loop (two one-way tracks) and an end station, making the stretch from the loop's far switch to the station one block (every signal between them dismantled, the loop's own signals kept) does not prevent the head-on deadlock of two trains: the train bound for the occupied station waits at the loop's exit signal with its front on the switch, and the train leaving the station cannot enter the other loop track. Where the waiting train stops decides, not the number of blocks. Evidence: both trains stood for over 220 s at signals 10 m ahead of them (`waiting at signal 262 s` and `223 s` in the status lines); with the signals as built they stood 261 s and 240 s the same way. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A train on a siding under a Path Signal whose way out leads through a junction to an occupied station track waits at the Path Signal (`RSA_Stop`). It moves 1–2 s after the station track is free. The mod needs no action of its own to leave the siding just in time. Evidence: <scenario>: `waits under the path signal while <station> behind the junction is taken: moved 0, path signal RSA_Stop, waiting at signal 21 s`, then `track free at 6.5 s, moved at 8.0 s of the stage`. (2026-10-12, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A signal at the very joint of a switch guards or observes more than one connection.** A siding's exit signal placed exactly where the siding joins the line (the merge) has `GetGuardedConnections()` of 2 (the end of the siding and the end of the line's track) and `GetObservedConnections()` of 1 (the track after the merge, whose connection has 2 connections). A signal placed where the tracks part (before the split) has 1 guarded and 2 observed. On a layout built the way players build (a Path Signal on the line before the split, block signals at the merge), the siding and the line beside it are then one Path Signal block from the split to the merge, so a "siding is a block of its own" rule refuses every such siding. Evidence: 52 signals of <test layout>: 8 with 1 guarded / 2 observed at splits, 7 with 2 guarded at merges, all 7 refused as "1 other track(s) in the block". A train standing on the siding branch at the switch does not let another train through the line branch (next two entries). (2026-10-13, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A train standing still inside a path block keeps every train whose path through that block overlaps the tracks it stands on, and the game counts the two tracks that converge into a switch as overlapping. Case: a siding whose exit signal stands at the switch where it joins the line, with a Path Signal before that switch on the siding and on the line, so the stubs of both before the switch are one path block. A train parked 8 m before the exit signal (its route ends there) stood on the siding's last track, and `GetOverlappingTracks()` of that track named the line's last track. Another train then stood at the Path Signal into the block on the line for 180 s (`next restrictive <path signal> RSA_Stop`, test stopped); with the parked train away, the same train drove the same way through the block in 7.4 s without a wait. So a parking place must not be inside a path block that the line beside it also uses near a switch; the header comment of `FFGRailroadSignalBlock` says the same in words ("If a train is parked inside a path block other trains cannot reserve a path that intersects with the parked train"). So a train parked on the siding branch at the switch does not let another train through the line branch. Evidence: `<scenario>`, kind "merge": `control done, A away: B is through the block 7.4 s after it began … stood at the signals into the shared block 0.0 s`, `A's vehicles stand on 1 tracks … tracks of the line the game counts as overlapping them: 1`, `B stood 180 s at <path signal> into it`. The earlier try waited 417 s at the same signal but had no run without the parked train to compare with. (2026-10-14, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- The same holds where the line splits: a siding with no entry signal of its own leaves the line at a switch inside a path block, its exit (parking) signal at the far end of its first track. A train parked 8 m before that exit signal stood on the siding's first track after the switch, which the game counts as overlapping the line's first track after the switch (`GetOverlappingTracks()`: 1).
