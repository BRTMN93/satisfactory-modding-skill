# Railways: signals, track connections, holding a train at a signal

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

## Signals and track connections

- **A signal between two tracks, seen from the connections.** Take a one-way block signal at the joint between track A (before the signal) and track B (behind it):
  - `GetGuardedConnections()` is A's connection at the joint. `GetObservedConnections()` is B's connection at the joint.
  - A's connection returns the signal from `GetFacingSignal()`. B's connection returns it from `GetTrailingSignal()`.
  - `GetObservedBlock()` is B's block (`AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()` of B has the same `ID`).
  - A connection with a trailing signal and no facing signal cannot be entered from that side. This is how a walk over the track graph sees a one-way signal from behind.
  - Evidence: a dump of 12 block signals and 37 tracks, for example `signal S03 … guards [T10.1], observes [T09.0], observed block 3`.
- **Signal block numbers are per track network.** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::ID` (through `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()`) starts again in every connected track network (`GetTrackGraphID()`): a save with four networks had a block 12 in three of them. Two tracks share a block only when both the block number and the track graph id are equal. Evidence: a dump listing tracks of networks 2, 11 and 15 all under `block 12`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **[?]** Track graph IDs (`GetTrackGraphID`) change when the player joins or splits networks; read them again each cycle instead of storing them per station. From a code review, not tested. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Tracks that cross without a joint are put into one signal block, and `GetOverlappingTracks()` names them.** On a double-track line a branch left one track through a switch and crossed the other track on the level. The crossed piece of the other track, fenced by its own entry path signal and exit block signal, had the same block number as the switch's tracks, the path signals of both tracks observed that one block (`IsPathBlock()`), and all signals were `RBV_Valid`. `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetOverlappingTracks()` of the crossed piece returned the branch's curve and the other way round. The two branches of every switch are listed as overlapping each other too (they lie close behind the point), which changes nothing because they share the switch's block anyway. **[?]** Two parallel tracks closer than 6 m (`RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE`) were not built. Evidence: `track T025 block 6 overlaps [T027 block 6]`, T025 joined only to its own line; `signal S50 path … observes [T025.0], observed block 6 (path block)`, `signal S51 path … observes [T028.0], observed block 6 (path block)`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Path signals as a train sees them.** A path signal shows `RSA_Stop` until an approaching train gets its reservation, so the autopilot brakes for it.
  - A train doing 100 km/h slowed to 51 km/h. The signal turned `RSA_Clear` when the train was 48 m in front of it, and the train accelerated again (same numbers on two laps).
  - At a path signal whose block was busy, the train stood 10.0 m in front of it and left when the signal cleared.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `T34.1 segment 7 48 m … RSA_Stop` and `… RSA_Clear` in the same tick.
- **Block signals and path signals open the same window.** `mInteractWidgetSoftClass` read from instances of `Build_RailroadBlockSignal_C` and `Build_RailroadPathSignal_C` is the same class, `/Game/FactoryGame/Buildable/Factory/Train/Signal/Widget_RailroadSignal.Widget_RailroadSignal_C`. So one widget hook on that class covers both kinds (hooks: `references/sml-api.md`, "SML: hooks"; the window's widget tree: `domains/railroad/ui-windows.md`). A path signal with no train near it shows `RSA_Stop` with `RBV_Valid`. Evidence: a dump of 9 block and 3 path signals.
- **After a save is loaded, the signals on the track connections are known in the first tick; the signal blocks and the trains' graph id are not.**
  - In the first tick every signal's guarded connection already returned that signal from `GetFacingSignal()` (and the observed one from `GetTrailingSignal()`), so a walk over tracks, switches and one-way signals works at once. A scenario found both sidings of a 46-track loop in its first tick, while all signals still showed `RSA_Stop` without a block.
  - In the first frame of the loaded world every signal reported `HasObservedBlock() == false`, `GetAspect() == RSA_Stop` and `GetBlockValidation() == RBV_Unvalidated`. `AFGTrain::GetTrackGraphID()` returned -1, while the track under the train's first vehicle already returned its graph id.
  - The block signals turned `RSA_Clear` one frame later (0.4 s on that load), so the wait for the blocks is about one frame, not two seconds. The first test saw all 12 signals with a block and `RBV_Valid`, and the trains returning the graph id, at frame 62 (about two seconds) without looking more finely; when exactly the trains' graph id comes was not measured.
  - Code that only needs the layout does not have to wait. Code that needs block ids or aspects waits for `HasObservedBlock()`, for example until every signal with `HasValidConnections()` has it. In the first frames it should take the graph id from a track, not from the train.
  - Evidence (game 502094): test log, frame 0: `12 of 12 signals observe no block yet`, `track graph -1, its first vehicle's track is in graph 2`. Frame 62: all valid. Second test: `[frame 0] t=0.0 sidings read 0.00 s after the load, in tick 1`, `[frame 0] signal S07 shows RSA_Stop`, `[frame 1] t=0.4 signal S07 shows RSA_Clear`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Replacing signals changed the track graph.** A player replaced three block signals with path signals, and the track graph came back with 38 tracks instead of 37. One 56.43 m track had become two, with the same summed length, and the new joint carried one of the new signals (game 502094).
  - A signal placed in the middle of a track piece makes the game split that piece into two track buildables (a players' tutorial for 1.0 says free placement "creates new segments"): a player-built signal removed a 48.00 m track actor and two new ones of 32.39 m and 15.61 m appeared, each announced by the buildable delegates (`references/game-classes.md`, "mBuildableRemovedDelegate"). Evidence: build events `gone [… 48.00 m]`, `new tracks [… 32.39 m …; … 15.61 m …]`, and 0 changes without a build event. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
  - So a mod's own track graph and any stored reference to a track must be refreshed when a signal is built, not only when tracks are.
- `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetIsOwnedByPlatform()` tells a platform's own track piece from the rest, and the station building's track is found through the connection's `GetStation()`; together they find the tracks a train stands on in a station (both ends of a docked train). Dismantling a signal from code (`IFGDismantleInterface::Execute_Dismantle(Signal)`) merges its two blocks: a mod graph rebuilt 2 s after the build event saw the block of the signal's track containing the tracks beyond the gone signal (packaged game, 2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0).

## How signals are placed and what the game checks

Rules for build lists, test layouts and layout checks. Collected from the game headers, the wiki and the signal guide hosted on the modding docs; the items with evidence were also read from a built layout with 22 signals, all `RBV_Valid`.

- **A signal is directional. One signal at a joint makes the joint one-way; a two-way track needs a pair at the same joint, one per direction.** In code: a signal that has a partner at its joint returns `IsBiDirectional() == true` and each of the two connections has one signal as `GetFacingSignal()` and the other as `GetTrailingSignal()`. A lone signal returns false, and the connection behind it has only a trailing signal, so nothing may pass it from that side. Evidence: `signal S07 block …, two-way 0, guards [T14.0]`, `T15.1 trailing S07`; `signal S13 block …, two-way 1`, `T18.0 facing S13 trailing S14`. Wiki: "If only one signal is present, it will prevent trains in the opposite direction from passing through." (https://satisfactory.wiki.gg/wiki/Train_Signals)
- **A signal placed at a switch sits on the switch's point and covers both branches**: it guards or observes two connections. Evidence: `signal S08 block …, guards [T14.1 T16.0], observes [T10.1]` and its partner `signal S21 path …, guards [T10.1], observes [T14.1 T16.0], observed block 4 (path block)`.
- **A block entered through path signals is a path block**: `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::IsPathBlock()` was true for exactly the blocks whose entry signals were path signals. **[source]** All entry signals of a block must be of one kind, exit signals may differ (wiki).
- **[source]** The block validation errors, enum `ERailroadBlockValidation` in `FGRailroadSignalBlock.h`, with the text the player sees (wiki): `RBV_NoExitSignals` "Block has no exit signal", `RBV_ContainsLoop` "Signal loops into itself" (the signal does not divide anything, also when two unconnected tracks lie too close: **[source]** the headers and a tutorial give 6 m; tracks 24 m apart had separate blocks; **[?]** tracks 8 m and 16 m apart, not connected, had separate blocks too, and an unconnected piece 4–8 m from a track was not reported as too close, distance not measured), `RBV_ContainsMixedEntrySignals` "Block has conflicting entry signal types", `RBV_ContainsStation` "Path block cannot contain stations". A signal at the very end of a track has no valid connections ("Signal has missing connections", `HasValidConnections()` false). A signal with an error counts as red.
- **[source]** A path signal is red until an approaching train has its path reserved; the reservation runs through every following path signal up to the next block signal, and the block behind that signal must be free ("trains will only enter a Path Segment if the following Block Segment is clear", signal guide on docs.ficsit.app). A train does not stop inside a path block. So: path signal on every entrance of a junction, block signal on every exit, and no path signal directly before a station (the station would be inside the path block). Players also put block signals on the stretches between junctions, so more than one train fits on a line: a layout built that way by a player had 22 signals, all valid, and two shuttle trains ran 5 courses each without an error (2026-10-04, game 502094, SML 3.12.0). **[?]** From the wiki and the community signal guide, read through a summary and not tested: a path block should be level or trains may collide; trains slow down for a red or erroring signal about 250 m ahead; a train driven by hand occupies its block, and path signals stay red for it; a signal sits at least 12 m from the end of a track piece; block spacing of 3–4 track lengths at most (wiki) or about 1.5 train lengths (guide). The sources disagree on the side of the track a signal must stand on ("Left Side/Right Side" build modes since 1.1 against "trains only look for signals on the right-hand side").
- **[source]** The game's pathfinding ignores other trains and takes the shortest route, so two two-way tracks side by side are no passing loop: both directions pick the same one. A passing loop needs each of its two tracks made one-way, with a single block signal behind the switch where a train enters the loop track and a single path signal before the switch where it leaves it (Steam discussion 6982351509012486495; the built layout above is signalled this way).
- **[source]** The modding docs have no page on the signal classes; their only railway text is the community signal guide (https://docs.ficsit.app/satisfactory-modding/latest/CommunityResources/TrainSignalGuide.html). The FicsIt-Networks reflection pages list `RailroadSignal` and `RailroadSignalBlock`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- A signal deep in a dead-end siding, facing the train that drives in, needs a second signal between it and the Buffer Stop or the game marks it `RBV_NoExitSignals` ("Block has no exit signal") and shows `RSA_Stop`: the stub it observes has no exit. With the second signal at the Buffer Stop the inner signal is `RBV_Valid` and the second one carries the error instead. A train whose route ends at the inner signal stops there either way (the route's end, not the aspect, stops it). This answers the **[?]** in `domains/railroad/own-routes.md` ("Whether the parking signal would be valid without it was not tried"): it is not. Evidence: dismantling the Buffer Stop signal from code turned the inner signal from `RBV_Valid` to `RBV_NoExitSignals, aspect RSA_Stop` within 5 s (packaged game, 2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0).

## Hook targets in the railway signalling

- **Hook targets in the railway signalling, verified.** All four installed in `POST_INITIALIZATION` with `SUBSCRIBE_METHOD` (plain structs, no sample object) and fired on the game thread:
  - `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::GetAspectFor`, a const method returning an enum: `[](auto& Scope, const FFGRailroadSignalBlock* Self, AFGBuildableRailroadSignal* Signal)`. `Scope(Self, Signal)` returns the game's aspect, `Scope.Override(ERailroadSignalAspect::RSA_Stop)` replaces it.
  - `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::CreatePathReservation`, returning a `TWeakPtr` by value: `[](auto& Scope, FFGRailroadSignalBlock* Self, AFGTrain* Train, AFGBuildableRailroadSignal* EntrySignal, int32 EntryPathSegment, FRailroadPathSharedPtr TrainPath)`. `CreateExclusiveReservation` is the same without the last argument.
  - `FFGRailroadBlockReservation::Approve`, a void method: `[](auto& Scope, FFGRailroadBlockReservation* Self)`. The train and the entry signal are public members of `Self` (`Train`, `EntrySignal`).
  - With the handlers rejecting every call in their first line, a train's pass showed the same aspects at the same distances and speeds as in a run without the hooks. Call rates in a world with three trains and 16 signals: `GetAspectFor` 1.3 a second, reservation creation and approval 0.25 a second each.
  - What these hooks do to trains: `domains/railroad/signals.md`, "Holding a train at a signal with hooks". Log lines: `Successfully hooked function FFGRailroadSignalBlock::GetAspectFor at …`, the same for the other three. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)

## Holding a train at a signal with hooks

A train with a whole route can be held at a signal by hooks in the signalling (the hooks' signatures are in `references/sml-api.md`, "SML: hooks"). A route that ends at the signal does the same without any hook and without the long-wait error (`SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal`; see "Parking a train at the end of a route, without touching the signals" above).

- **How a signal gets its aspect, and how to make it read it again.** A signal caches its aspect (`AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::GetAspect()`). It takes a new one from the block it observes, through `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::GetAspectFor(signal)`, when that block reports a change.
  - The game calls `GetAspectFor` on changes, not every tick: about 1.3 calls a second in a world with three running trains and 16 signals (965 calls in 743 s).
  - The block's change delegate is public, so `Signal->GetObservedBlock().Pin()->OnBlockChanged.Broadcast()` makes every signal of that block read its aspect at once.
  - The autopilot obeys the cached aspect, whatever the block's real state is.
  - Evidence: right after the broadcast a hook on `GetAspectFor` had been called once for that signal, `GetAspect()` returned the hook's value (`signal shows RSA_Stop, the game's own aspect RSA_Clear; aspect asked 1 times`), and a train in front of that signal stood there for 900 s. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Holding a train with a forced stop aspect (hook on `GetAspectFor`).** A "before" hook called the original and overrode the result with `RSA_Stop` for one signal, followed by the broadcast above. A train with a whole route through that signal stopped 10.0 m before it, at a path signal and at a block signal, and stayed (0.00 m of drift in holds up to 335 s, `TimeWaitingAtSignal` counting). When the override was lifted (and the block broadcast again), the train moved 1.0 s later if the block behind was free. What it costs:
  - `GetAspectFor` gets no train, because an aspect belongs to the signal. Every train in front of that signal is held. In one run the hook was armed while another train still stood in the block before the signal: that train never left, the train meant to be held could not enter the block, and the whole loop stood until the test's time limit. Arm such a hook only when no other train is in that block.
  - Behind a **block signal** nothing else happens. The game made no reservation for the waiting train (no `CreatePathReservation` or `CreateExclusiveReservation` call with that train and entry signal in 335 s), so the block's other entries stayed open: 7 passes over the neighbouring track next to the held train, none stopped, the same speeds as with no train held.
  - Behind a **path signal** the game still reserves. It created the train's path reservation while the train approached (about 17 s before the train stood) and approved it a few seconds after the stop, once the junction was free. The forced aspect hides the approval from the train, but the reservation keeps the junction: a train on the crossing route stood 58 s at its own path signal, until the hold was lifted. A forced stop alone must not be used at a path signal.
  - After 300 s the held train gets `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal`, as at any signal, and the player sees the warning. The game does not look for another route. The error cleared when the train got going.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `stopped 10.0 m before parking signal …, whole train in the siding's block 1, signal shows RSA_Stop`, `reservations made by the game 0` (block signal, 335 s), `kept: path approved in block 8` with `… waits 20 s at signal … (RSA_Stop), its block 8 occupied 0` (path signal), `t=328.0 event … error SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` after a stop at t=28. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Holding a train at a path signal by taking its reservation back (hook on `CreatePathReservation`).** The handler called the original, cancelled the new reservation (`Pin()->Cancel()`) and overrode the result with an empty handle, for one train and one entry signal (both are arguments, so the filter is exact).
  - The path signal stayed at stop by itself (the game's own aspect was never clear) and the train stopped 10.0 m before it. The aspect is not touched.
  - Trains on the crossing route were not disturbed: 8 passes next to the held train in holds of 20 s and 330 s, no standstill, the same speeds as with no train held.
  - The scheduler asks again every frame: 20 879 reservations created and cancelled in 330 s (63 a second), and each one makes the junction's signals read their aspects (two `GetAspectFor` calls a frame). No crash and no error apart from `SDLE_LongWaitAtSignal` after 300 s.
  - When the hook let the reservation through again, the game approved it as usual: the train moved 1.0 s later with a free junction, or once the train in its way had gone (24–28 s).
  - **[source]** At a block signal there is nothing to take back (see above), and a block signal shows clear while its block is free, so this hook alone does not hold a train there. This hook was run at a path signal only; the block signal case follows from the run with the forced aspect.
  - **[?]** `AFGTrainScheduler` gives out reservation numbers from a counter that rolls over at 100 000 (`mReservationCounterRolloverAt`). If every re-created reservation takes a number, such a hold rolls it over about every 27 minutes at 60 fps. Not tested: the longest hold was 335 s.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `aspect asked 20916 times (the game said clear 0 times), forced to stop 0; reservations made by the game 20879, taken back 20879`, `main line passes next to a train held by R at a path signal: 7, lowest speed 14 km/h, longest standstill 0.0 s`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Withholding the approval of a reservation does not free the junction (hook on `FFGRailroadBlockReservation::Approve`).** `Scope.Cancel()` for one train and entry signal left its path reservation pending: the path signal stayed at stop and the train was held 10.0 m before it.
  - The scheduler called `Approve` again every frame once the junction was free (2003 calls in a 40 s hold). But the pending reservation kept the junction just like an approved one: a train on the crossing route stood 58 s at its path signal with the junction's block free, until the hook was lifted. Then the game approved at once and the held train moved after 1.0 s.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `kept: path pending in block 8; approvals asked 2003, withheld 2003`, `… passed over the main line … in 80.4 s, lowest speed 0 km/h, stood 58.3 s`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Reservations and a hooked aspect are not part of a save.** A train saved while it was held by the hooks above, autopilot on, came back in the same place (0.00 m off) with no route, and the game found its route in the second tick.
  - With the hold armed again in the mod's first tick after the load, before the signal blocks exist (the signal came from a saved reference), the train stayed for the 30 s tested.
  - At a block signal the block was built in the second tick and asked for the aspect once: the game said clear, the hook answered stop, and the signal never showed clear.
  - At a path signal the reservation was taken back 1764 times.
  - A train held with the autopilot on and nothing armed leaves about a second after a load ("A train's route is not saved").
  - Evidence (game 502094): `hooks armed again … in tick 1 after the load: signal shows RSA_Stop, the game's own aspect RSA_None; aspect asked 0 times`, `[t=0.3] hooks …: signal shows RSA_Stop, the game's own aspect RSA_Clear; aspect asked 1 times … forced to stop 1`, `released … after 30 s …: drift while held 0.00 m`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
