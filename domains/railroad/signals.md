# Railways: signals, track connections, holding a train at a signal

Part of the `satisfactory-modding` skill, railroad domain (index: `domains/railroad/README.md`). Markers: no marker = verified in practice, **[source]** = read from SML or game code, not tested, **[?]** = uncertain. Replace the parts in `<...>` with your own.

## Signals and track connections

- **A signal between two tracks, seen from the connections.** Take a one-way block signal at the joint between track A (before the signal) and track B (behind it):
  - `GetGuardedConnections()` is A's connection at the joint. `GetObservedConnections()` is B's connection at the joint.
  - A's connection returns the signal from `GetFacingSignal()`. B's connection returns it from `GetTrailingSignal()`.
  - `GetObservedBlock()` is B's block (`AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()` of B has the same `ID`).
  - A connection with a trailing signal and no facing signal cannot be entered from that side. This is how a walk over the track graph sees a one-way signal from behind.
  - Evidence: a dump of 12 block signals and 37 tracks, for example `signal S03 … guards [T10.1], observes [T09.0], observed block 3`.
- **Signal block numbers are per track network.** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::ID` (through `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock()`) starts again in every connected track network (`GetTrackGraphID()`): a save with four networks had a block 12 in three of them. Two tracks share a block only when both the block number and the track graph id are equal. Evidence: a dump listing tracks of networks 2, 11 and 15 all under `block 12`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Track graph IDs (`GetTrackGraphID`) change when networks are joined or split; read them again each cycle instead of storing them per station.** A track joining two networks gave the second network the first one's ID (36 to 31); dismantling it gave one half a new ID. Evidence: `<scenario> SPLIT 2 s after the dismantle: graph IDs 1 1 1 1 gone 31 31 31 31`. (2026-10-26, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Tracks that cross without a joint are put into one signal block, and `GetOverlappingTracks()` names them.** On a double-track line a branch left one track through a switch and crossed the other track on the level. The crossed piece of the other track, fenced by its own entry path signal and exit block signal, had the same block number as the switch's tracks, the path signals of both tracks observed that one block (`IsPathBlock()`), and all signals were `RBV_Valid`. `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetOverlappingTracks()` of the crossed piece returned the branch's curve and the other way round. The two branches of every switch are listed as overlapping each other too (they lie close behind the point), which changes nothing because they share the switch's block anyway. Two parallel tracks closer than 6 m (`RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE`) share a block as well, see the next entry. Evidence: `track T025 block 6 overlaps [T027 block 6]`, T025 joined only to its own line; `signal S50 path … observes [T025.0], observed block 6 (path block)`, `signal S51 path … observes [T028.0], observed block 6 (path block)`. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Parallel tracks 5 m apart share a block, 6 m apart do not (closes the [?] on `RAILROAD_BLOCK_OVERLAP_DISTANCE`).** A Railway piece laid parallel to a straight track, not joined to it, its middle 5.00 m from the other track: the game listed the two as overlapping (`GetOverlappingTracks()`), gave both the same block number, and put the loose piece into the other track's graph (`GetTrackGraphID()`). Pairs of tracks without a common joint whose closest points lay 5.97 m and 6.56 m apart (beside switches) had different blocks. Distances measured by sampling both splines every 2 m (`GetWorldLocationAndDirectionAtPosition`), so up to about 0.2 m long. A siding check that reads the game's blocks needs no distance rule of its own. Evidence: `new track …: 56.00 m … net 0, block 13, the game lists as overlapping [… net 0 block 13, 5.00 m from the new track's middle]`; `… 5.97 m apart, blocks 12 and 13 (apart)`. (2026-10-13, game 502094)
- **Path signals as a train sees them.** A path signal shows `RSA_Stop` until an approaching train gets its reservation, so the autopilot brakes for it.
  - A train doing 100 km/h slowed to 51 km/h. The signal turned `RSA_Clear` when the train was 48 m in front of it, and the train accelerated again (same numbers on two laps).
  - At a path signal whose block was busy, the train stood 10.0 m in front of it and left when the signal cleared.
  - Evidence (game 502094): `T34.1 segment 7 48 m … RSA_Stop` and `… RSA_Clear` in the same tick.
- **Block signals and path signals open the same window.** `mInteractWidgetSoftClass` read from instances of `Build_RailroadBlockSignal_C` and `Build_RailroadPathSignal_C` is the same class, `/Game/FactoryGame/Buildable/Factory/Train/Signal/Widget_RailroadSignal.Widget_RailroadSignal_C`. So one widget hook on that class covers both kinds (hooks: `references/sml-api.md`, "SML: hooks"; the window's widget tree: `domains/railroad/ui-windows.md`). A path signal with no train near it shows `RSA_Stop` with `RBV_Valid`. Evidence: a dump of 9 block and 3 path signals.
- **After a save is loaded, the signals on the track connections are known in the first tick; the signal blocks and the trains' graph id are not.**
  - In the first tick every signal's guarded connection already returned that signal from `GetFacingSignal()` (and the observed one from `GetTrailingSignal()`), so a walk over tracks, switches and one-way signals works at once. A scenario found both sidings of a 46-track loop in its first tick, while all signals still showed `RSA_Stop` without a block.
  - In the first frame of the loaded world every signal reported `HasObservedBlock() == false`, `GetAspect() == RSA_Stop` and `GetBlockValidation() == RBV_Unvalidated`. `AFGTrain::GetTrackGraphID()` returned -1, while the track under the train's first vehicle already returned its graph id.
  - The block signals turned `RSA_Clear` one frame later (0.4 s on that load), so the wait for the blocks is about one frame, not two seconds. The first test saw all 12 signals with a block and `RBV_Valid`, and the trains returning the graph id, at frame 62 (about two seconds) without looking more finely; when exactly the trains' graph id comes was not measured.
  - Code that only needs the layout does not have to wait. Code that needs block ids or aspects waits for `HasObservedBlock()`, for example until every signal with `HasValidConnections()` has it. In the first frames it should take the graph id from a track, not from the train.
  - Evidence (game 502094): test log, frame 0: `12 of 12 signals observe no block yet`, `track graph -1, its first vehicle's track is in graph 2`. Frame 62: all valid. Second test: `[frame 0] t=0.0 sidings read 0.00 s after the load, in tick 1`, `[frame 0] signal S07 shows RSA_Stop`, `[frame 1] t=0.4 signal S07 shows RSA_Clear`. (2026-10-03, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **Replacing signals changed the track graph.** A player replaced three block signals with path signals, and the track graph came back with 38 tracks instead of 37. One 56.43 m track had become two, with the same summed length, and the new joint carried one of the new signals (game 502094).
  - A signal placed in the middle of a track piece makes the game split that piece into two track buildables (a players' tutorial for 1.0 says free placement "creates new segments"): a player-built signal removed a 48.00 m track actor and two new ones of 32.39 m and 15.61 m appeared, each announced by the buildable delegates (`references/game-classes.md`, "mBuildableRemovedDelegate"). Evidence: build events `gone [… 48.00 m]`, `new tracks [… 32.39 m …; … 15.61 m …]`, and 0 changes without a build event. (2026-10-05, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
  - So a mod's own track graph and any stored reference to a track must be refreshed when a signal is built, not only when tracks are.
- `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetIsOwnedByPlatform()` tells a platform's own track piece from the rest, and the station building's track is found through the connection's `GetStation()`; together they find the tracks a train stands on in a station (both ends of a docked train). Dismantling a signal from code (`IFGDismantleInterface::Execute_Dismantle(Signal)`) merges its two blocks: a mod graph rebuilt 2 s after the build event saw the block of the signal's track containing the tracks beyond the gone signal (packaged game, 2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0).
- Dismantling one signal of a pair that stands on both sides of a joint leaves the other one as a one-way signal: a train that would have to pass it from behind gets `ESelfDrivingLocomotiveError::SDLE_StationUnreachableWithSignals` as soon as its autopilot asks for the route, and the leg ends with no route. Evidence: a path signal dismantled from code (`IFGDismantleInterface::Execute_Dismantle`) with its block signal partner kept, `ends: ESelfDrivingLocomotiveError::SDLE_StationUnreachableWithSignals` 5 s after the leg began. (2026-10-11, game 502094, SML 3.12.0)
- **A signal's lamps are not a mesh of their own.** `AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::mSignalComponent` (protected, a `UFGColoredInstanceMeshProxy`) shows one static mesh with one section and one material (path signal: `SM_TrainSignalType02_01` right-handed, `_02` left-handed, material `MI_TrainSignalType_2`; the block signal's Blueprint names `SM_TrainSignalType01_01`/`_02` **[source]**), and the lamps' colours come from the material through the signal's visual state (custom data), so a lamp cannot be shown, hidden or recoloured apart from the rest of the signal. The mesh has no CPU access (`bAllowCPUAccess` 0), so its triangles cannot be read either. The path signal's mesh box is X −75…360, Y ±112, Z −59…1104 cm (about 11 m high), the component sits at relative location (0, 450, 0) with yaw −90. Evidence: a dump of 6 path signals' mesh components on a test save. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **A signal's head can be found from its mesh's collision boxes, in the cooked game.** `GetStaticMesh()->GetBodySetup()->AggGeom.BoxElems` of the signal's mesh component gives unrotated boxes in the mesh's space; the box that reaches highest is the top of the head, and line traces against the signal alone (`AActor::ActorLineTraceSingle`, `ECC_Visibility`) hit the same boxes. Measured, mesh space in cm: Block Signal head x −59.3…169.1, y ±19.2, top 1113.8 (lamps look along Y); Path Signal boxes near the top y ±58.3 together, top 1085–1095. A component added to the signal (a `UStaticMeshComponent` with `/Engine/BasicShapes/Cube`, attached to the signal's mesh component, `RF_Transient`, no collision) stays out of the save and shows on every machine that makes it. Evidence: three cubes turned 0/60/120° about one axis drawn as a hexagonal prism on both signals, three parts after switching on, none after switching off, after a save and a load again three. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The game's signal lamp material is in the cooked game and takes a colour.** `/Game/FactoryGame/Buildable/Factory/Train/Signal/MI_TrainSignal_Green` loads with `LoadObject<UMaterialInterface>`; its one vector parameter is `Color`, and a `UMaterialInstanceDynamic` with `Color` (0, 0, 1) shows a glowing light blue. Evidence: the log line of the material and screenshots of the shape on two signals. (2026-10-23, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **The signal lamp material glows, and its `Color` is an emissive value: the game's lamps use about 1.** `MI_TrainSignal_Green` has `Color` (0.0107, 1, 0), `MI_TrainSignal_Red` (1, 0.015, 0) (`GetVectorParameterValue`). A copy with another colour at a brightest channel of 1 or more turns almost white on screen by day (pure blue at 4.3 looked almost white), at 0.1–0.2 it keeps its hue (blue 0.1 showed #0000C8, violet #8000FF 0.2 showed #A938E5). At night the eye adaptation brightens everything, the game's own red and green lamps turn pale too, and the same blue 0.1 showed #3C76F1, violet 0.2 #DABDFC. The exposure adapts over seconds, so a screenshot is judged about 15 s after changing the time of day (`AFGTimeOfDaySubsystem::SetDaySeconds`). Evidence: screenshots of a mark in this material on a <Path Signal> and a <Block Signal> at several values by day and at midnight, colour sampled at the mark's centre. (2026-10-23, game CL 513885)
- `AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::mIsLeftHanded` (`UPROPERTY(SaveGame, Replicated)`, read with `IsLeftHanded()`) is enough to make a signal left-handed for a test: it is set by reflection (`FindFProperty<FBoolProperty>(AFGBuildableRailroadSignal::StaticClass(), TEXT("mIsLeftHanded"))`) on the signals of <scenario>, the save is stored under a new name, and after loading that save the game builds the left-hand meshes itself. Evidence: after the load the log shows the meshes `SM_TrainSignalType01_02` (Block Signal) and `SM_TrainSignalType02_02` (Path Signal) and `IsLeftHanded()` 1 on the changed signals; the right-hand ones keep `..._01`. Setting the field in a running world without a save and load does not swap the mesh **[?]** (not tried). (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** `GetGuardedConnections()` returns `TArray<UFGRailroadTrackConnectionComponent*>` by value (a copy of `mGuardedConnections`, `FGBuildableRailroadSignal.h:75`). A range-for over the call is fine, but `const auto& List = Signal->GetGuardedConnections();` keeps only a temporary alive and a pointer into it dangles once the statement ends; in hot code it is called once per signal, because it allocates each time. (2026-10-24, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Signal side and direction from the build-gun hologram:** the side (left/right of the track) is the hologram's build mode, the one R cycles: `SetBuildModeOverride(<mode with "Left" or "Right" in its class name>)` + `OnBuildModeChanged(mode)`. The direction the signal faces (which trains it guards) is the mouse wheel: `Hologram->ScrollRotate(1, Hologram->GetRotationStep())`, then place again. The user pointed this out; built that way, block and path signals came out on the wanted side and, after one scroll where needed, facing the wanted trains (checked by the guarded connection's normal against the trains' way). Evidence: <scenario>, `signal <X> (path, left): path, left, faces the trains it should` after `dismantled, built again (try 1: turned …)`. (2026-10-25, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Whether a train is in a plain block can be read in O(1) from the track under it: `AFGBuildableRailroadTrack::GetSignalBlock().Pin()` and the block's own flags.** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock` has `IsPathBlock()`, `ContainsStation`, `ContainsLoop`, `ContainsMixedEntrySignals`, `NoExitSignal` and `IsOccupied()`; a block whose flags are all false (and the entry signal a one-way block signal: `!IsPathSignal()`, `!IsBiDirectional()`, `GetBlockValidation() == ERailroadBlockValidation::RBV_Valid`) is an ordinary block between a block signal and its exit signal, with no walk over the tracks. On a 768 m block between two one-way block signals with no station all five flags read false and `IsOccupied()` true while a train stood in it; two tracks belong to the same block when `GetSignalBlock().HasSameObject(Block)`. **[source]** What makes each flag true (a platform, a loop, two entries of different kinds, a block without an exit signal) is read from the header names, not tested one by one. Evidence: `the long block E-X 768.0 m (a plain block)` in a test's start line, the block test passing for that block in each of the 24 runs that let a train in and refusing other blocks on a large save (`the signal is no valid one-way block signal at stop`, 14 times). (2026-11-03, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **[source]** **A path signal shows clear for an approved reservation whatever train asks, so it does not keep a second train out of the path block behind a first one.** `FFGRailroadSignalBlock::GetAspectFor` returns Clear for a path block whenever an approved reservation has that signal as its entry (the header: "For path blocks, multiple inbound signals might be green at the same time"), and a train makes its own reservation only while the signal shows Stop to it. So for a train following another closely, the path signal at the exit of the followed block is no stop point of its own until the follower holds an approved reservation. Evidence: the game's disassembly and the header comment, read in a code review; no in-game test yet. (2026-11-03, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
- **Confirmation of "parallel tracks 5 m apart share a block" for a track of another network, with a train in it.** A one-way track with no joint to the line (its own network, own stations and Block Signals), laid 5 m beside the line for 48 Foundations: a train driven by the player past that track's red Block Signal at 120 km/h was reported inside the line's block (the block of the line's train standing there) while it drove the shared part, and outside it once past. So a follower supervised inside a block meets such a train only through the block's occupants, not through the trains on the follower's route. Evidence: <scenario> `W in the block E-X on its own track (the game gives the two tracks one block), 120.1 km/h`, then `W out of the block and stopped`, 0 contacts. (2026-11-04, game 513885, SML 3.12.0)
